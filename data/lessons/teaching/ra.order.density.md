# Density of the Rationals

**Density:** between any two distinct real numbers there is a rational number, and between any two there is also an irrational, so neither the rationals nor the irrationals leave any open interval untouched.

## Rationals between any two reals

Let a be less than b. The gap is positive, so by the Archimedean property there is an integer n with one over n less than the gap. The multiples of one over n form a grid of step one over n, which is finer than the gap, so some grid point m over n lies strictly between a and b. That grid point is rational. This proves density in one line, and it shows that the finer the required approximation the larger the denominator used.

## Irrationals between any two reals

The irrationals are dense for the same reason, with one extra step. Take a rational point strictly inside the interval and add an irrational multiple of one over n that is small enough to stay inside. The sum of a rational and an irrational is irrational, so the result is an irrational number inside the interval. Density of the irrationals is not a separate theorem; it is density of the rationals plus the fact that adding a suitably small irrational preserves the interval.

## Consequences

Density says that no open interval is free of rationals, and no open interval is free of irrationals. Applying it to a subinterval gives infinitely many of each. It also shows the rationals are not discrete: there is no smallest positive gap between them. Density alone does not make the rationals complete, and the two properties must not be confused: the rationals are everywhere dense yet still full of gaps, which is exactly why completeness is needed alongside density.
