# Triple-Angle Formulas

**Triple-angle:** identities for the sine, cosine and tangent of three times an angle, obtained by applying the double-angle formulas to the angle plus twice the angle.

## Sine of three times an angle

The sine of three times an angle expands to three times the sine minus four times the cube of the sine. The derivation writes the triple angle as the angle plus twice the angle, applies the sum formula, and then replaces the double-angle terms with their expressions in the single angle. Only the sine appears in the result, which is what makes the formula useful: the sine of the triple is a polynomial in the sine of the single angle.

## Cosine and tangent

The cosine of three times an angle is four times the cube of the cosine minus three times the cosine, the companion polynomial with alternating signs. The tangent of the triple angle is three times the tangent minus the cube of the tangent, all divided by one minus three times the square of the tangent, obtained by dividing the sine formula by the cosine formula. The three formulas together show that the trigonometric functions of a multiple angle are polynomials in the function of the single angle.

## Why the polynomials matter

These formulas are the reason the trigonometric functions of a rational multiple of a turn are algebraic numbers, and they are the historical route to the cubic equation that governs the trisection of an angle. A triple angle set equal to a known value becomes a cubic in the single-angle function, and the three roots are the three angles that share that triple. The same polynomials appear when a signal is passed through a device that cubes it, because the output contains the third harmonic with exactly these coefficients.
