# Nonlinear Systems

**Nonlinear system:** a system in which at least one equation is not linear; it is solved by substitution, which produces an equation that may have several solutions.

## Substitution

Nonlinear systems are usually solved by solving the simpler equation for one variable and substituting into the other. The result is a single equation in one variable, often a quadratic, whose solutions are substituted back to find the paired values. Every solution of the quadratic gives a point of the system, so the answer is a list of pairs rather than a single pair.

## Several solutions

Because a quadratic can have two roots, a nonlinear system can have more than one solution, and each root of the substituted equation yields a distinct point. The geometric picture matches: a line and a parabola can meet in two points, one, or none. Every candidate pair must be checked in both original equations, since squaring or substituting can introduce pairs that satisfy only the transformed system.

## Choosing the easier variable

When one equation is already solved for a variable, substituting directly is the shortest route, and when both are quadratic it is often easier to subtract the equations to eliminate a square. Choosing the substitution that avoids a fourth-degree equation keeps the work within reach. The aim is always to reduce the system to one equation in one variable that can be solved by factoring or the quadratic formula.
