# The Extended Real Line

**Extended real line:** the real numbers together with two extra symbols, plus and minus infinity, ordered so that every real lies between them and every set has a supremum and an infimum.

## Adjoining two symbols

The extended real line adds plus infinity and minus infinity to the reals. The order is extended so that minus infinity is below every real and plus infinity is above every real. The arithmetic is only partly extended: adding a real to plus infinity gives plus infinity, and multiplying plus infinity by a positive real keeps the sign, but expressions such as plus infinity minus plus infinity and zero times infinity are left undefined. The two symbols are order objects first and algebraic objects second.

## Order completeness

In the extended line every subset has a supremum and an infimum. A set that is bounded above has its ordinary real supremum; a set that is unbounded above has plus infinity as its supremum, and an unbounded-below set has minus infinity. The empty set is given supremum minus infinity and infimum plus infinity by convention. This convention is what lets theorems about suprema be stated without a separate unbounded case, at the price of allowing an infinite value.

## Where it is used

Limits of sequences are often written in the extended line: a sequence increasing without bound is said to converge to plus infinity, and the convention lets the limit notation cover divergence to an endpoint. Measures of sets are values in the extended nonnegative line, so an unbounded set may have infinite measure. In each case the extended symbol is a convenience that replaces a case distinction with a single extended equation, and the undefined combinations mark the places where that convenience stops.
