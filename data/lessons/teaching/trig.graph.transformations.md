# Transformations of Sinusoidal Graphs

**Sinusoidal transformation:** the effect of the four parameters in a scaled, shifted sine or cosine, which set the amplitude, the period, the phase shift and the vertical shift.

## The four parameters

A sinusoidal graph is written with a coefficient in front, a coefficient inside the angle, a constant subtracted inside the angle and a constant added outside. The outside coefficient sets the amplitude, the absolute value of the height above the midline. The inside coefficient sets the period, 360 degrees divided by its absolute value. The inside constant sets the phase shift, the horizontal displacement, and the outside constant sets the vertical shift of the midline.

## Order of application

Transformations are applied in a fixed order: the inside operations act on the input first, compressing and shifting it horizontally, then the outside coefficient stretches the result vertically, and the outside constant moves it up or down. Because the horizontal shift is inside the coefficient, a graph described as a shift of c actually moves by c divided by the inside coefficient. Reading the shift correctly requires factoring the inside coefficient out of the angle first.

## Recovering an equation from a graph

From a graph, the amplitude is half the distance between a maximum and a minimum, the midline is their average, the period is the distance between corresponding points, and the phase shift is where a chosen feature sits relative to the origin. The inside coefficient follows from the period, and the outside coefficient from the amplitude and the midline. A sine and a cosine describe the same graph with different phase shifts, which is why more than one correct equation exists.
