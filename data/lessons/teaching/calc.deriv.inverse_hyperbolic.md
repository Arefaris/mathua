# Inverse Hyperbolic Derivatives

**Inverse hyperbolic derivatives:** the derivatives of arsinh, arcosh and artanh, obtained by inverting the hyperbolic functions and using the identity that connects them.

## Inverting the hyperbolic functions

The inverse hyperbolic functions undo sinh, cosh and tanh. Their derivatives follow from the inverse-function rule: if the input is the hyperbolic function of the output, then the derivative of the inverse is the reciprocal of the derivative of the original, evaluated at the output expressed in terms of the input. The hyperbolic identity that the square of cosh exceeds the square of sinh by one then converts that reciprocal into a form in the input alone. The results are all algebraic, with square roots and rational expressions, even though the original functions involve exponentials.

## The three rules

The derivative of arsinh is one over the square root of one plus the square of the input, valid for every real input because the expression under the root is always positive. The derivative of arcosh is one over the square root of the square of the input minus one, valid where the input exceeds one, which is where arcosh is defined. The derivative of artanh is one over one minus the square of the input, valid inside the interval from minus one to one. Each domain matches the range of the original hyperbolic function, which is where the inverse is real.

## Recognising them in integrals

These rules are most often used backwards, as integration formulas. The integral of one over the square root of one plus the square of the input is arsinh of the input, and the integral of one over one minus the square of the input is artanh of the input. Recognising the pattern saves a trigonometric substitution and gives the answer directly in hyperbolic form. The same three expressions, with the root of a quadratic in place of a single square, are the standard first step in integrating reciprocal quadratics.
