# Graph of the Tangent Function

**Tangent graph:** the graph of the tangent repeats every 180 degrees, crosses zero at whole turns and half turns, and has vertical asymptotes where the cosine vanishes.

## Shape and period

The tangent is the sine divided by the cosine, so it rises from zero at a whole turn, increases without bound as the angle approaches ninety degrees, and reappears on the other side of the asymptote at minus infinity. One full branch runs from minus ninety to plus ninety, and the whole picture repeats every 180 degrees because both the sine and the cosine change sign under a half turn and their ratio does not. The graph has no amplitude, since the tangent is unbounded.

## Asymptotes and zeros

The vertical asymptotes sit at the angles where the cosine is zero, which are ninety degrees plus whole multiples of 180. The zeros sit at the angles where the sine is zero, which are whole multiples of 180, so each branch crosses the axis once at its midpoint. Between an asymptote and the next, the tangent increases strictly from minus infinity to plus infinity, which makes each branch one-to-one and invertible on that interval.

## Transformations

A coefficient inside the angle compresses the branches, and the period becomes 180 divided by the absolute value of that coefficient. A coefficient in front stretches the graph vertically without moving the asymptotes, and a constant added shifts the whole picture up or down. A constant added inside the angle shifts the asymptotes and the zeros together, which is why the phase shift of a tangent graph is read from where a zero lands rather than from a peak.
