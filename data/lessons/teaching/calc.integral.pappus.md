# Pappus's Centroid Theorem

**Pappus's centroid theorem:** When a plane region of area $A$ is revolved about an external axis in its plane that does not cut the region, the volume swept is $V = 2\pi d A$, where $d$ is the distance from the centroid to the axis. The same theorem gives a surface area when a curve of length $L$ is revolved: $S = 2\pi d L$.

## Revolving a region about an external axis

The centroid travels a circular path of circumference $2\pi d$, and the swept volume is the area times the distance that its centroid travels. The theorem converts a solid-of-revolution integral into a product, provided the axis is external and the region does not cross it.

## Worked: a rectangle and a triangle

1. A rectangle of area 6 with its centroid 5 units from the axis sweeps $2\pi \cdot 5 \cdot 6 = 60\pi$.
2. A triangle of area 4 with centroid 3 units from the axis sweeps $2\pi \cdot 3 \cdot 4 = 24\pi$.
3. A region of area 10 with its centroid 1 unit from the axis sweeps $20\pi$.

## The conditions that make it valid

The axis must lie outside the region, in the same plane, and must not cut it; otherwise the revolution self-intersects and the product no longer counts distinct volume. The centroid distance is measured perpendicular to the axis. The theorem is a shortcut, not a replacement for the disk or shell method, and the two must agree whenever both apply.
