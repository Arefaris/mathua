# Derivatives of Hyperbolic Functions

**Hyperbolic derivative:** the derivative of the hyperbolic sine is the hyperbolic cosine, the derivative of the hyperbolic cosine is the hyperbolic sine, and the derivative of the hyperbolic tangent is the hyperbolic secant squared.

## The two basic derivatives

Differentiating the definitions in terms of exponentials gives the hyperbolic sine derivative as the hyperbolic cosine and the hyperbolic cosine derivative as the hyperbolic sine, with no sign change in either. This is the one place the hyperbolic functions are simpler than the trigonometric ones, where differentiating the cosine produces a minus sine. The absence of the sign change is what makes the hyperbolic functions the natural solutions of the equation in which a function equals its own second derivative.

## The quotient derivatives

The hyperbolic tangent is the hyperbolic sine over the hyperbolic cosine, so the quotient rule gives the hyperbolic secant squared, which is also one minus the hyperbolic tangent squared. The hyperbolic cotangent gives minus the hyperbolic cosecant squared, the hyperbolic secant gives minus the secant times the tangent, and the hyperbolic cosecant gives minus the cosecant times the cotangent. The signs follow the pattern of the trigonometric derivatives but with the roles of the two basic functions exchanged.

## Chain rule and uses

Composing a hyperbolic function with an inner function multiplies the derivative by the inner derivative, exactly as for any other function. The derivatives are what make the hyperbolic functions useful in integration, because an integral of a hyperbolic sine is a hyperbolic cosine with no sign to correct. They also appear in the solutions of the wave and heat equations on unbounded domains, where the exponential growth of the hyperbolic functions matches the growth of the solutions.
