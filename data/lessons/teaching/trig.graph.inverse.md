# Graphs of Inverse Trigonometric Functions

**Inverse trigonometric graph:** the graph of an inverse trigonometric function is the original graph reflected across the line y equals x, restricted to the interval on which the original is one-to-one.

## Why the domain is restricted

The trigonometric functions repeat, so none of them is one-to-one on the whole line and none has an inverse there. Restricting the sine to the interval from minus ninety to ninety degrees makes it strictly increasing and therefore invertible, and the inverse sine takes values in exactly that interval. The cosine is restricted to the interval from zero to one hundred eighty degrees, where it decreases strictly, and the tangent to the open interval from minus ninety to ninety.

## Reading the three graphs

The inverse sine has domain from minus one to one and range from minus ninety to ninety, and its graph passes through the origin with vertical tangents at the endpoints. The inverse cosine has the same domain but range from zero to one hundred eighty, so it decreases from one hundred eighty at minus one to zero at one. The inverse tangent has the whole line as domain and the open interval from minus ninety to ninety as range, with horizontal asymptotes at both ends.

## Composition and caution

The inverse functions undo the originals only on the restricted interval: the sine of the inverse sine of a value returns the value, but the inverse sine of the sine of an angle returns the angle only when the angle lies in the restricted range. Outside that range the composition returns the coterminal angle inside the range, which is the reflection of the periodicity restriction. This is the same care that any inverse of a non-injective function requires.
