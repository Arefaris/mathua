# Simplifying Complex Fractions

**Complex fraction:** a fraction whose numerator or denominator is itself a fraction; it is simplified by rewriting the division as a multiplication by the reciprocal.

## Dividing is multiplying by the reciprocal

A fraction divided by a fraction is the product of the first and the reciprocal of the second, so a complex fraction collapses to a single fraction in one step. The numerator goes on top of the result and the denominator's numerator and denominator swap below. Clearing the inner fractions this way is faster and less error-prone than multiplying every part by a common denominator.

## Combining the inner parts first

When the numerator or denominator is a sum of fractions rather than a single fraction, it must first be written over a common denominator. Only after each part is a single fraction does the reciprocal rule apply. Doing the work in this order avoids the common mistake of inverting a sum term by term, which is not valid.

## Keeping track of restrictions

Every simplification of a complex fraction carries hidden restrictions, because each denominator that appears forbids a value of the variable. These restrictions must be recorded even after the factor that caused them cancels, since the original expression was undefined there. The final simplified form is equal to the original on the common domain, and the domain is the intersection of all the denominators' admissible values.
