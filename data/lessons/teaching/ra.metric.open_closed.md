# Open and Closed Sets

**Open and closed:** a set is open when every point has a ball around it inside the set, and closed when its complement is open; in a metric space arbitrary unions and finite intersections of open sets are open.

## Open sets

A subset of a metric space is open when every one of its points has an open ball, of some positive radius, contained entirely in the set. Open balls are themselves open, and the open sets are exactly the unions of balls. The empty set and the whole space are open, an arbitrary union of open sets is open, and a finite intersection of open sets is open, because the smallest of the finitely many radii can be chosen. An arbitrary intersection of open sets need not be open, as shrinking balls about a point show.

## Closed sets

A set is closed when its complement is open, which is equivalent to containing all of its limit points: a point is a limit point when every ball around it meets the set in some other point. Closed sets are closed under arbitrary intersections and finite unions, by the complementary laws from the open case. On the line the closed intervals are closed, and a set can be neither open nor closed, or both, since the empty set and the whole space are both.

## Relative and absolute

Openness depends on the ambient space. The interval from zero to one is open in itself but meets the whole line as a set that is not open there, and the same interval is closed as a subset of itself. To keep this straight, one speaks of relatively open sets, open in the subspace with the restricted metric. The subspace topology consists of exactly the intersections of the ambient open sets with the subspace, and every statement about open and closed sets is understood relatively when a subset is the whole world.
