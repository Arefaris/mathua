# Trigonometric Inequalities on an Interval

**Trigonometric inequality:** an inequality between trigonometric expressions, solved by finding the intervals of one period where it holds and then repeating or restricting them.

## Solving on one period

An inequality in a trigonometric function is solved by marking the angles where the two sides are equal and then testing one point in each interval between them. On one period the equality points cut the circle into arcs, and the sign of the difference is constant on each arc. Testing a single convenient angle in each arc decides the whole arc, which is why the equality points must be found first even though they are not themselves solutions of a strict inequality.

## Reading the arcs

A sine greater than one half holds between thirty and one hundred fifty degrees in the first period, an interval that straddles ninety. A cosine less than zero holds between ninety and two hundred seventy degrees, the half turn centred on one hundred eighty. A tangent at least one holds from forty-five degrees up to, but not including, ninety, and again one period later. Each interval is read from the unit circle rather than from a graph alone.

## Extending and restricting

On the whole line, each interval is repeated by whole periods, which produces infinitely many intervals and is usually written with an integer in the endpoints. On a stated interval, the repeated intervals are intersected with it, and only the pieces inside survive. The answer is written as a union of intervals, and the endpoints are open or closed according to whether the inequality is strict and whether the function is defined there.
