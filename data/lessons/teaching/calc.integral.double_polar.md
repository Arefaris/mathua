# Double Integrals in Polar Coordinates

**Double integral in polar coordinates:** an integral over a region described by radius and angle, with area element the product of radius and the differentials of radius and angle.

## The area element

Describing a region by radius and angle changes the area element from the product of the coordinate differentials to the product of the radius, the differential of the radius and the differential of the angle. The extra factor of the radius is the length of the arc element: a small rectangle in Cartesian coordinates becomes a small sector in polar coordinates, and its area is proportional to the radius. Forgetting this factor is the single most common error in a polar integral, and it is why the area of a disk is not simply the product of its radius and its circumference without thought.

## The disk and other regions

For a disk centred at the origin the radius runs from zero to its maximum and the angle runs over a full turn, so the integral separates into two one-dimensional integrals and the standard area results follow immediately. For a region between two curves the radial limits are the two curves expressed in polar form and the angular limits are where they meet. For a region outside one circle and inside another the order of integration can be chosen to keep the limits simple, which is the reason polar coordinates exist.

## When to choose polar

Polar coordinates are chosen when the region or the integrand has rotational symmetry: circles, annuli, sectors, and integrands that depend only on the distance from the origin. A multiple of the square root of the sum of the squared coordinates is the standard signal, because it becomes the radius alone. The same factor of the radius appears in the triple-integral analogues for cylindrical and spherical coordinates, with different angular factors, and the largest single simplification in those systems is the same one of matching the coordinates to the symmetry.
