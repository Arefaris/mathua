# The Strong Law of Large Numbers

**Strong law of large numbers:** independent, identically distributed observations with a finite mean have a sample mean that converges to that mean almost surely.

## The almost-sure statement

The strong law strengthens the weak law by making the convergence almost sure rather than merely in probability. For almost every outcome the running averages settle down to the population mean, not just typically. The conclusion is therefore about the whole sequence at once.

## The hypotheses

Independence, identical distribution and a finite mean are enough. A finite variance is not required, even though it makes the proof easier. This economy of assumptions is one of the reasons the strong law is regarded as the definitive statement about sample averages.

## Failure without a mean

When the mean does not exist the strong law can fail. The Cauchy distribution is the standard example: it is symmetric, yet its sample average never settles down, because the heavy tails keep producing large observations that reset the running mean. A finite mean is not a technical convenience; it is what the theorem is about.
