# The Beta Function

**Beta function:** the two-argument integral whose value at positive integers is a ratio of factorials, closely related to the gamma function and to binomial coefficients.

## The definition and the factorial form

The beta function is defined by an integral over the unit interval of the argument powers, and for positive integer arguments it equals the product of the factorials of one less than each argument, divided by the factorial of the sum of the arguments minus one. Reading it as a ratio of factorials makes it computable by hand for small integers and explains its symmetry: it does not change when the two arguments are swapped, because the definition and the factorial form are symmetric. It is the continuous analogue of the reciprocal of a binomial coefficient.

## The gamma relation

The beta function equals the product of the gamma functions of the two arguments divided by the gamma function of their sum. This identity converts every factorial ratio into a gamma ratio and extends the function to non-integer arguments. It is proved by changing variables in the product of two gamma integrals and identifying a beta integral in the result. The relation is the reason the beta function appears wherever beta-like integrals or binomial-looking coefficients arise.

## Where it is used

The beta function evaluates integrals of powers of the variable and of one minus the variable, which appear in probability, in the arcsine and beta distributions, and in the normalisation of the Student distribution. It also gives the value of the integral of a power of the sine or cosine over a half period, which is the standard reduction formula for those powers. Recognising a beta integral is usually faster than integrating by parts repeatedly.
