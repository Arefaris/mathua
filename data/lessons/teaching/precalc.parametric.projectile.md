# Projectile Motion

**Projectile motion:** With gravity and no air resistance, horizontal and vertical motion separate. The height is $y(t) = h + v_y t - \frac12 g t^2$ and the horizontal position is $x(t) = x_0 + v_x t$, so the path is a parabola.

## Separate horizontal and vertical motion

Gravity acts only vertically, so the horizontal velocity stays constant while the vertical velocity decreases by $g$ each second. The two equations share the time $t$, and eliminating it gives the parabolic path. Near the surface, $g \approx 9.8$ m/s², often rounded to 10 for clean numbers.

## Worked: maximum height

1. A projectile is launched vertically at $v = 20$ m/s with $g = 10$ m/s².
2. The height is $y = 20t - 5t^2$.
3. The vertex is at $t = v/g = 2$, and $y(2) = 40 - 20 = 20$ m.

## Range and time of flight

For a launch at angle $\theta$, $v_x = v\cos\theta$ and $v_y = v\sin\theta$. The time of flight is $2v_y/g$ when the landing height equals the launch height, and the range is $v_x$ times that time, $v^2\sin(2\theta)/g$. The maximum range at fixed speed occurs at 45 degrees.
