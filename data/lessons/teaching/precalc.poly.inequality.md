# Polynomial and Rational Inequalities

**Sign analysis:** A polynomial or rational expression can change sign only at its zeros and at values where it is undefined. Mark those points on a number line, test one value in each interval, and read off where the inequality holds.

## Signs between the roots

For a product of linear factors, each factor changes sign at its root. Between two simple roots the product has one sign, and outside them the other. A repeated root does not change the sign, which is why multiplicity matters.

## Worked: a quadratic inequality

1. Solve $(x-1)(x-3) < 0$.
2. The roots are 1 and 3; testing $x = 2$ gives $(1)(-1) = -1 < 0$.
3. So the solution is the open interval $(1,3)$, of width 2.

## Rational inequalities and excluded values

For a rational inequality, the denominator's zeros are excluded even when the inequality is non-strict. The sign chart then uses both the numerator's and the denominator's zeros. For example, $(x-1)/(x-3) > 0$ is positive outside the roots, on $(-\infty,1) \cup (3,\infty)$, and $x = 3$ is never in the solution set.
