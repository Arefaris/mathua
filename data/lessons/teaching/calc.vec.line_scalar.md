# Scalar Line Integrals

**Scalar line integral:** the integral of a scalar function along a curve, with the arc length element, giving the total of the function weighted by length.

## The arc length element

A scalar line integral accumulates the values of a function along a curve, weighting each small piece by its length. When the curve is given parametrically the arc length element is the length of the velocity vector times the differential of the parameter, so the integral becomes an ordinary one-variable integral of the function times that speed. Unlike the flux and work integrals, the scalar line integral does not care about the direction of travel, because it depends only on the arc length element, which is nonnegative.

## Computing along a segment

Along a straight segment from the origin the coordinates are constant multiples of the parameter and the speed is constant, so the integral of a coordinate reduces to the integral of a linear function over the unit parameter interval. The value is the arc length times the average of the coordinate along the segment. This is why the scalar integral of a coordinate over a straight segment is a simple product, and why the Pythagorean lengths of common segments reappear in the answers.

## Mass, average and arc length

Taking the function to be one gives the arc length of the curve, the simplest scalar line integral. Taking it to be a density per unit length gives the total mass of a wire, and dividing by the arc length gives the average density. The scalar line integral therefore generalises the length of a curve in the same way that the definite integral generalises the length of an interval, and it is the prerequisite for the work integral, which adds direction.
