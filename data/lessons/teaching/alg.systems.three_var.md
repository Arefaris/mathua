# Systems of Three Equations

**System of three equations:** three linear equations in three unknowns; it is solved by eliminating one unknown at a time until a single equation in one unknown remains.

## Elimination in three variables

The method is elimination repeated: combine two pairs of equations to remove the same variable, leaving a system of two equations in two unknowns, then solve that and back-substitute. The choice of which variable to remove first is free, and it is worth choosing the one that produces the simplest coefficients. Each step is the two-variable elimination already known, applied carefully to keep the equations paired.

## Back-substitution

Once one unknown is known, its value is substituted into the earlier two-variable equations to find a second, and both go back into an original equation to find the third. Working backwards through the equations is why the method is called back-substitution. Checking the solution in all three original equations catches any arithmetic slip, since a correct triple satisfies every one of them.

## What the solution means

A triple that satisfies all three equations is a point common to three planes in space, and the geometry mirrors the cases of two dimensions: the planes may meet in a single point, along a line, or not at all. One solution means one common point, infinitely many mean a common line or plane, and none mean the planes are arranged so that no point lies on all three. The algebra and the picture agree at every step.
