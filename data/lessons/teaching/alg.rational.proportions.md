# Solving Proportions

**Proportion:** an equation stating that two ratios are equal; it is solved by cross-multiplying, which turns the proportion into a linear equation.

## Cross-multiplication

A proportion equates two fractions, and multiplying both sides by the product of the denominators clears them, giving the product of the first numerator and the second denominator equal to the product of the first denominator and the second numerator. The resulting equation is linear when each term is a simple expression, so the unknown is isolated by dividing. This single move replaces the fraction equation with ordinary algebra.

## Setting up the proportion

The hardest part of a proportion is usually writing it, not solving it. The two ratios must compare the same two quantities in the same order, so the units line up diagonally when the proportion is written correctly. Checking that each ratio has the same units above and below is the standard safeguard against an inverted setup.

## Direct and inverse variation

A direct proportion says two quantities grow together, so their ratio is constant; an inverse proportion says one grows as the other shrinks, so their product is constant. Cross-multiplication solves both, but the setup differs: the direct case sets up equal fractions, and the inverse case sets the two products equal. Reading the physical situation tells you which form is right.
