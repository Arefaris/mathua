# Vector Identities

**Vector identities:** the algebraic relationships among gradient, divergence and curl, above all that the divergence of a curl and the curl of a gradient both vanish.

## The two vanishing composites

The divergence of the curl of any smooth field is zero, and the curl of the gradient of any smooth scalar field is zero. Both statements follow from the equality of mixed partial derivatives: the operators combine second derivatives in a way that cancels pairwise, because swapping the order of differentiation does not change the result. The two identities are the reason the gradient fields and the curl fields form separate families, the irrotational and the solenoidal fields, and every further identity is built from them.

## Reading the language

Curl zero means the field has no local rotation and, on a simply connected domain, is a gradient field, so its line integrals are path-independent. Divergence zero means the field has no sources or sinks and is a curl field locally. The two operators are adjoint to the gradient under the integral formulas, which is why Green's identities, the divergence theorem and Stokes's theorem all express one in terms of the others. The vocabulary of potentials depends entirely on these two vanishing composites.

## A capstone to the domain

These identities collect the vector calculus of the domain into a small algebra: the gradient turns a scalar into a vector, the divergence and curl turn a vector into a scalar and a vector, and the compositions that make sense either vanish or produce the Laplacian. The three integral theorems are then the statements that the operators integrate by parts, moving a derivative from the field to the weight. Recognising an identity is what lets a surface integral be recognised as a volume integral and so be evaluated.
