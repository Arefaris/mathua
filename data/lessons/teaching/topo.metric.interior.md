# Interior, Closure and Boundary

**Three regions:** For a set $A$ in a space $X$, the interior is the largest open set inside $A$, the closure is the smallest closed set containing $A$, and the boundary is the set of points every neighbourhood of which meets both $A$ and its complement.

## The three regions of a set

The interior and the boundary are disjoint, the closure is the union of the interior and the boundary, and every point of the space falls into exactly one of the interior, the exterior and the boundary. For the open interval $(0,1)$ in $\mathbb{R}$, the interior is $(0,1)$, the boundary is $\{0,1\}$, and the closure is $[0,1]$.

## Worked: the boundary of a closed interval

1. Take $A = [0, 2]$ in $\mathbb{R}$.
2. Near 0 every ball reaches negative numbers and points of $A$, so 0 is a boundary point.
3. Near 2 the same happens, and every other point is interior; the boundary is $\{0, 2\}$, two points.

## Interior points versus limit points

An interior point has a ball entirely inside $A$; a limit point has every ball meeting $A$ at another point, but the ball may leave $A$. In $[0,2]$ every point is a limit point, while only the points strictly between 0 and 2 are interior. An isolated point such as 1 in $\{1,2,3\}$ is neither interior nor a limit point.
