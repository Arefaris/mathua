# General Flux Integrals

**Flux integral:** the integral of the normal component of a vector field over an oriented surface, measuring the total flow through the surface.

## Normal components

The flux of a vector field through a surface is the integral over the surface of the component of the field normal to the surface, per unit area. Only the part of the field perpendicular to the surface carries through it, so the flux is the field dotted with the unit normal, integrated with the surface element. The orientation, the choice of which way the normal points, is part of the data: reversing it reverses the sign of the flux, and a closed surface is conventionally oriented outward.

## Constant fields and flat surfaces

For a constant field and a flat surface the normal is the same everywhere, so the dot product with the field is a constant and the flux is that constant times the area. Only the field component along the normal matters, and the components parallel to the surface contribute nothing because they lie along it. This is the simplest case of the flux integral and the one that gives the intuition for the general formula, in which the normal varies and the field may vary with position.

## The divergence theorem

The divergence theorem turns the flux of a field through a closed surface into the integral of its divergence over the enclosed solid, which is often easier. When the divergence is zero the field is solenoidal and the flux through any closed surface is zero, being the difference of what flows in and what flows out. The scalar surface integral, the flux integral and the divergence theorem form a ladder, and each rung is used in turn to convert a surface problem into a volume problem or the reverse.
