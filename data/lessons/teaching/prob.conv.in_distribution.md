# Convergence in Distribution

**Convergence in distribution:** a sequence converges to a limit law when the distribution functions converge at every point where the limit function is continuous.

## Weak convergence of laws

Convergence in distribution constrains the laws, not the variables. The classical definition asks for pointwise convergence of the distribution functions away from the discontinuity points of the limit, and an equivalent formulation asks that the expectation of every bounded continuous function converge. It is the weakest mode, and it is the one limit theorems usually establish.

## What it controls and what it does not

Because only the marginal laws are constrained, the joint behaviour of the variables is invisible. A sequence can converge in distribution yet fail to converge in probability when the variables are not tied to a common limit on one probability space. This is why theorem statements say weak convergence and then need a separate argument for stronger modes.

## The central limit theorem as its central example

The central limit theorem says that standardised sums converge in distribution to the standard normal. Each summand's law is irrelevant beyond its mean and variance, and the limit is stated through the distribution functions. Convergence in distribution is the native mode of the central limit theorem and of most asymptotic statements about laws.
