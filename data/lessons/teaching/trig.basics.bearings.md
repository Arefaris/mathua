# Bearings and Navigation

**Bearing:** a direction given as an angle measured clockwise from north, always between 0 and 360 degrees, so a course can be stated without reference to a coordinate grid.

## Measuring from north

A bearing fixes the starting direction at north and measures clockwise, so north is 0 degrees, east is 90, south is 180 and west is 270. The clockwise convention means a bearing is never negative and never exceeds 360, which makes two courses easy to compare. A bearing names a ray from the observer, so it describes a direction of travel rather than a position, and the return journey from the destination has a different bearing.

## Converting from a described course

Many problems describe a course as a turn from north or south toward east or west, such as thirty degrees east of north. That description converts to a bearing by measuring from north clockwise: a course east of north keeps the angle, a course east of south is 180 minus the angle, a course west of south is 180 plus the angle, and a course west of north is 360 minus the angle. Each case places the ray in one quadrant, and the four formulas are the four quadrants written out.

## Bearings in triangle problems

Bearings turn navigation into trigonometry because a course and a distance form a side of a triangle. Two legs of a journey with known bearings give the included angle between them, and the law of cosines then gives the direct distance back, while the law of sines gives the bearing of the return leg. The return bearing is the outbound bearing plus or minus 180 degrees, reduced into the first full turn, which is the same reversal that coterminal angles describe.
