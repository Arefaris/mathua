# Continuity and Its Algebra

**Continuity:** a function is continuous at a point when its limit there equals its value there, and continuous on a set when it is continuous at every point of the set.

## The definition

A function is continuous at a point when the limit of the function as its input approaches the point exists, is finite, and equals the value of the function at the point. Continuity on a set means continuity at every point of the set. The definition can be read in terms of epsilon and delta without the punctured condition: a small change in the input produces a small change in the output. Discontinuities come in kinds, from removable jumps to raising the value at one point while the limit stays fixed.

## The algebra of continuity

Continuity is preserved by the usual operations. If two functions are continuous at a point then their sum, difference and product are continuous there, and their quotient is continuous there provided the denominator does not vanish. The composition of continuous functions is continuous, because a small change in the inner output produces a small change in the outer value. These rules are proved by transferring the algebra of limits through the sequential criterion, and they assemble the continuity of any expression built from continuous parts.

## Elementary functions

Polynomials are continuous everywhere because they are built from the identity and constants by sums and products. Rational functions are continuous wherever their denominators do not vanish, so their discontinuities sit exactly at the zeros of the denominator. The exponential is continuous on the whole line, and the logarithm on the positive reals, while the trigonometric functions are continuous where they are defined. Recognising an elementary function as continuous on an interval is the usual first step before quoting a theorem that needs continuity there.
