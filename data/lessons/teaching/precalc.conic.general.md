# Classifying a General Conic

**General conic:** An equation $Ax^2 + Bxy + Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$. The discriminant $B^2 - 4AC$ decides the type: negative for an ellipse (or circle), zero for a parabola, positive for a hyperbola.

## The discriminant decides the type

The sign of $B^2 - 4AC$ is invariant under rotation of the axes, so it classifies the conic even when the cross term $Bxy$ is present. The circle is the special ellipse with $A = C$ and $B = 0$; a degenerate case can collapse to a point or a pair of lines.

## Worked: ellipse, parabola and hyperbola

1. $2x^2 + 3xy + 4y^2 + \cdots = 0$ has $B^2 - 4AC = 9 - 32 = -23$, so it is an ellipse.
2. $x^2 + 2xy + y^2 + \cdots = 0$ has $4 - 4 = 0$, so it is a parabola.
3. $x^2 - y^2 + \cdots = 0$ has $0 + 4 = 4$, so it is a hyperbola.

## Rotation and the cross term

A nonzero $B$ means the axes of the conic are rotated relative to the coordinate axes. Rotating by the angle that removes the cross term puts the equation in standard form, and the discriminant is unchanged by that rotation. That invariance is why the sign alone is enough to classify the curve.
