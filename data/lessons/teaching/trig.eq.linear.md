# Linear Trigonometric Equations

**Linear trigonometric equation:** an equation in which a trigonometric function appears only to the first power, solved by isolating the function and then reading the angles.

## Isolate the function

A linear trigonometric equation has the shape of a constant times a function equal to a constant, possibly with further terms that are also linear in the same function. The first move is always algebraic: collect the terms in the function and divide, exactly as a linear equation in a variable is solved. The result is a statement that the function equals a number, and only then does trigonometry begin.

## Read the angles

If the isolated value is outside the range of the function, the equation has no solution, which is the first thing to check. If it is inside, the reference angle comes from the special angles or from an inverse function, and the quadrants come from the sign. The sine is positive in the first and second quadrants and negative in the third and fourth, the cosine is positive in the first and fourth, and the tangent is positive in the first and third. The smallest nonnegative solution is the base angle in the lowest quadrant that matches the sign.

## Worked reading

An equation that twice the sine equals one isolates a sine of one half, whose smallest nonnegative solution is thirty degrees. An equation that twice the sine equals minus one isolates a sine of minus one half, whose smallest nonnegative solution is two hundred ten degrees, in the third quadrant. An equation that twice the sine equals two isolates a sine of one, giving ninety degrees, and one that isolates a sine of zero gives zero. Each case is decided by the sign and by the range.
