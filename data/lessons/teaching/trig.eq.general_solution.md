# General Solutions of Trigonometric Equations

**General solution:** the complete set of angles that satisfy a trigonometric equation, written as the solutions in one period plus whole multiples of the period.

## Why one cycle is not the answer

A trigonometric equation usually has infinitely many solutions, because the functions repeat. Solving in one period gives the base angles, and adding whole periods to each gives the complete set. The period added is 360 degrees for sine and cosine equations and 180 degrees for tangent equations, and each base angle gets its own family. Writing only the base angles is the commonest error, because it reports a fraction of the answer.

## The two-family pattern

An equation in the sine or cosine with a value strictly between minus one and one has two base angles in a full turn, one in each of two quadrants. Each base angle generates its own family, and the two families are distinct even when the base angles look related, as thirty and one hundred fifty degrees do for a sine of one half. The tangent equation has one base angle in a half turn, so it generates a single family with a period of 180 degrees.

## Writing the answer

A general solution is written as the base angle plus the period times an integer, and the integer is understood to range over all whole numbers, negative ones included. Some textbooks write the cosine families as plus or minus a base angle, which is the same set written more compactly. The answer is complete when every solution of the equation appears in exactly one of the families, which is the test to apply before finishing.
