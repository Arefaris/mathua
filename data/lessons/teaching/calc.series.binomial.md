# The Binomial Series

**Binomial series:** the expansion of one plus the variable raised to a power, whose coefficient of the k-th power is the generalised binomial coefficient.

## The generalised coefficients

The binomial series expands one plus the variable raised to a real exponent as a power series whose coefficient of the k-th power is the product of the exponent and all integers below it down to the exponent minus k plus one, divided by k factorial. For a nonnegative integer exponent the product hits zero after finitely many factors, so the series is the ordinary binomial expansion and terminates. For other exponents the series continues forever and must be justified by convergence. The coefficients are read off one at a time by the same pattern.

## Convergence and the radius

The binomial series converges for inputs strictly inside the unit interval, by the ratio test applied to its coefficients, and the radius is one whatever the exponent. At the two endpoints the behaviour depends on the exponent: it converges for an exponent above minus one, and diverges otherwise. This is the same radius as the geometric series because the coefficients grow like a fixed power of the index. Inside the radius the series is equal to the power of one plus the variable, which is the identity that makes the expansion useful.

## Where it is used

The binomial series gives the expansions of square roots and other fractional powers, such as the square root of one plus the variable near zero, which is used to expand relativistic corrections and small-parameter physical formulas. The coefficients can also be read as ordinary binomial coefficients with a real top, using factorials replaced by the gamma function. The first few terms are the ones usually quoted, and the remainder is bounded by the next term when the series alternates.
