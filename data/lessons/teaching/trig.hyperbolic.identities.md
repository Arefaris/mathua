# Hyperbolic Identities

**Hyperbolic identity:** an identity among the hyperbolic functions, mirroring a trigonometric identity with occasional sign changes that come from the minus sign in the defining difference.

## The basic identity

The hyperbolic cosine squared minus the hyperbolic sine squared equals one, for every real input. The identity follows directly from the definitions in terms of exponentials, because the product of the two exponential terms cancels in the difference and leaves one. This is the hyperbolic analogue of the Pythagorean identity, and every other hyperbolic identity is derived from it by division, exactly as the trigonometric variants are.

## The divided forms

Dividing the basic identity by the hyperbolic cosine squared gives one minus the hyperbolic tangent squared equals the hyperbolic secant squared, with the minus sign where the trigonometric version has a plus. Dividing by the hyperbolic sine squared gives the hyperbolic cotangent squared minus one equals the hyperbolic cosecant squared. The sign difference is the single systematic change between the two families, and it comes from the subtraction in the defining identity.

## Addition formulas

The hyperbolic sine of a sum is the sine of the first times the cosine of the second plus the cosine of the first times the sine of the second, matching the trigonometric addition formula exactly. The hyperbolic cosine of a sum is the cosine of the first times the cosine of the second plus the sine of the first times the sine of the second, with a plus where the trigonometric version has a minus. These two formulas generate the double-angle and multiple-angle identities for the hyperbolic functions.
