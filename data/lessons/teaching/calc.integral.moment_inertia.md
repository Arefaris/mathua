# Moment of Inertia

**Moment of inertia:** The resistance of a mass distribution to angular acceleration about an axis, $I = \int r^2\,dm$, where $r$ is the perpendicular distance from the axis. For discrete masses, $I = \sum m_i r_i^2$.

## Resistance to rotation

The farther the mass sits from the axis, the larger its moment of inertia: the same mass at twice the distance contributes four times as much. This is why a hollow cylinder is harder to spin up than a solid one of the same mass.

## Worked: a rod, a disk and point masses

1. A uniform rod of mass 12 and length 2 about its centre has $I = ML^2/12 = 4$.
2. The same rod about one end has $I = ML^2/3$, four times larger.
3. A uniform disk of mass 4 and radius 2 about its central axis has $I = \frac12 MR^2 = 8$.
4. Point masses 2 and 3 at distances 1 and 2 from an axis give $I = 2(1)^2 + 3(2)^2 = 14$.

## Choosing the axis

The moment of inertia depends on the axis, so the axis must be stated with the problem. The parallel-axis theorem moves a known moment from the centroid to a parallel axis: $I = I_{cm} + Md^2$. For a rod, that relation turns $ML^2/12$ about the centre into $ML^2/3$ about an end.
