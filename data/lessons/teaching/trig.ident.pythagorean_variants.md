# Variants of the Pythagorean Identity

**Pythagorean variants:** the three identities that relate each trigonometric function to its cofunction pair, obtained by dividing the basic identity by a square.

## The three forms

Dividing the basic identity that the sine square plus the cosine square is one by the cosine square gives the tangent square plus one equals the secant square. Dividing by the sine square gives one plus the cotangent square equals the cosecant square. Subtracting the tangent square from both sides of the first gives the secant square minus the tangent square equals one, which is the same identity rearranged for use in the other direction.

## Choosing a form

The form to use is the one whose functions already appear in the expression being simplified. An expression with secants and tangents is reduced with the secant identity, one with cosecants and cotangents with the cosecant identity, and one with a mixture of sines and cosines with the basic identity. Each variant is an exact identity wherever both sides are defined, so a substitution never changes the value, only the appearance.

## Domain caveats

The variants inherit the restrictions of the functions they contain. The secant and tangent forms exclude the angles where the cosine is zero, and the cosecant and cotangent forms exclude the angles where the sine is zero, so an identity proved by division holds only where the divisor is nonzero. This is why a simplification can appear to hold at an excluded angle and must be checked separately there, which is the same care an algebraic cancellation of a factor requires.
