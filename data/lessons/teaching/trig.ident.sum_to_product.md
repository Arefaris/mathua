# Sum-to-Product Formulas

**Sum-to-product:** identities that rewrite a sum or difference of two sines or cosines as a product, the inverse direction of the product-to-sum formulas.

## The four sums

A sum of two sines is twice the sine of the average angle times the cosine of the half difference. A difference of two sines is twice the cosine of the average times the sine of the half difference. A sum of two cosines is twice the cosine of the average times the cosine of the half difference, and a difference of two cosines is minus twice the sine of the average times the sine of the half difference. Every one of them uses the average and the half difference of the two angles.

## Deriving them

Each identity follows from the product-to-sum formulas by substituting the average and half difference for the two angles and solving for the sum. Writing the two angles as the average plus the half difference and the average minus the half difference turns their sum into twice the average and their difference into the difference itself. Adding or subtracting the product-to-sum pair then leaves the sum or difference on one side, which is the identity.

## Uses

Sums of sines and cosines are what an interference pattern actually produces, so the sum-to-product direction is the one that reveals the envelope. Two tones of nearly equal frequency sum to a single tone at the average frequency whose amplitude varies at the half difference, which is the beat. In equations, a sum equal to zero becomes a product equal to zero, which splits into the separate cases that the zero-product rule can solve.
