# Product-to-Sum Formulas

**Product-to-sum:** identities that rewrite a product of sines and cosines as a sum or difference of sines and cosines, trading multiplication for addition.

## The three products

Three identities cover every product of two trigonometric factors. The product of a sine and a cosine is half the sum of the sine of the sum and the sine of the difference. The product of two cosines is half the sum of the cosine of the difference and the cosine of the sum. The product of two sines is half the cosine of the difference minus the cosine of the sum. Each is read directly from the sum and difference formulas by adding or subtracting them, which is why the factors of one half appear.

## Reading the formulas

The pattern to remember is that a product of different functions becomes a sum of sines, and a product of like functions becomes a difference or sum of cosines. The arguments are always the sum and the difference of the two original angles, in that order for the cosine of a difference and in either order for the rest. Signs follow from the subtraction: the sine product carries a minus, because the two sum formulas subtract in the opposite order from the cosine case.

## Where they are used

Products of trigonometric functions appear whenever two oscillations multiply, as in amplitude modulation, beats between two sound waves, and the integrands of products of sines and cosines. Converting to a sum turns an integral of a product into a sum of integrals that the basic rules handle immediately. The same conversion explains beats: the product of two nearby frequencies becomes a fast oscillation whose amplitude is a slow cosine, and the slow factor is the beat.
