# Periodicity of Trigonometric Functions

**Periodic:** a function that repeats its values at a fixed spacing, called its period; the sine and cosine repeat every 360 degrees and the tangent every 180.

## What a period is

Periodicity means that the whole graph repeats. For a function of an angle, the period is the smallest positive angle after which the values begin again: adding that angle to the input changes nothing about the output. The sine and the cosine have period 360 degrees, or two pi radians, because one full turn brings the terminal side back to where it started. The tangent has period 180 degrees, because a half turn reverses both coordinates and their ratio is unchanged.

## Period from the coefficient

When the angle is multiplied by a constant, the graph is compressed or stretched horizontally. The function of b times the angle completes one cycle when the argument advances by 360 degrees, so the input need only advance by 360 divided by b. A coefficient of two halves the period to 180 degrees, a coefficient of three thirds it to 120, and a coefficient of a half doubles it to 720. The coefficient must be positive in this reading; a negative coefficient reflects the graph and the period uses its absolute value.

## Using the period

Knowing the period is what makes a table of values enough to describe the whole graph. Values in one cycle can be shifted by whole periods to cover every angle, and the general solution of a trigonometric equation is written the same way, by adding whole periods to the solutions found in one cycle. Periodicity is also the reason a trigonometric function cannot be one-to-one on the whole line, which is why the inverse functions are defined on a restricted interval of one period.
