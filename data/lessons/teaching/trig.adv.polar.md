# Polar Coordinates

**Polar coordinates:** a way to name a point by its distance from the origin and the angle of the ray to it, instead of by horizontal and vertical distances.

## The two coordinate systems

A polar coordinate pair gives a radius and an angle, and the point lies that far along the ray at that angle. The radius is measured from the origin and the angle from the positive horizontal axis, counterclockwise. The same point has infinitely many polar names, because adding a whole turn to the angle changes nothing, and a negative radius points the opposite way along the ray. Cartesian coordinates are unique, so the passage between the systems needs care.

## Converting to Cartesian

The horizontal coordinate is the radius times the cosine of the angle and the vertical coordinate is the radius times the sine of the angle, which is just the definition of the sine and cosine on a circle of that radius. Converting back, the radius is the square root of the sum of the squares and the angle is the inverse tangent of the ratio, placed in the correct quadrant by the signs of the two coordinates. The inverse tangent alone loses the quadrant, so the signs must be used as well.

## Exact values

At the special angles the cosine and sine are exact fractions, so a polar point converts without a calculator. An angle of sixty degrees has a cosine of one half, so a radius of six gives a horizontal coordinate of three, and an angle of one hundred twenty degrees has a cosine of minus one half, giving minus three for the same radius. Choosing the radius to clear the denominator keeps the answer an integer, which is why the exercises use multiples that divide evenly.
