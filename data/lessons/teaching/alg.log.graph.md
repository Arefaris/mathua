# Graphs of Logarithmic Functions

**Logarithmic function:** the inverse of an exponential function, defined for positive inputs, with a vertical asymptote at zero and a graph that reflects the exponential across the diagonal.

## The reflection of the exponential

The logarithm undoes the exponential, so its graph is the exponential graph reflected across the diagonal line where the two coordinates are equal. Where the exponential had a horizontal asymptote the logarithm has a vertical one, and where the exponential passed through a point with height one the logarithm passes through the point with input one and height zero. The domain is the positive numbers, matching the positive range of the exponential.

## Growth without bound

For a base greater than one the logarithm increases without bound as its input grows, but so slowly that it is eventually smaller than any positive power of the input. It is decreasing for a base between zero and one, which is the reflection of a decaying exponential. The curve is concave throughout and passes through two fixed points worth memorising: the input one gives height zero, and the input equal to the base gives height one.

## Building the graph

A table of values built from powers of the base gives a handful of exact points, because the logarithm reports the exponent needed to reach the input. The vertical asymptote at zero and the two fixed points are enough to sketch the shape, and the reflection of the exponential supplies the rest. The same curve appears whenever a quantity grows slowly enough that each doubling adds only a fixed amount, which is the pattern behind several logarithmic scales.
