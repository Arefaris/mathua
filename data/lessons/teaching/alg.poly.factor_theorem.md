# The Factor Theorem

**Factor theorem:** a polynomial has the linear factor x minus r exactly when its value at r is zero, so roots and factors are two views of the same fact.

## Roots and factors

Dividing a polynomial by a linear factor leaves a remainder equal to the polynomial evaluated at the corresponding number, a fact known as the remainder theorem. When that remainder is zero the division is exact and the linear expression is a factor. The factor theorem is this statement specialised to a zero remainder, and it lets a root be tested either by substitution or by division.

## Finding factors

To test whether a given linear expression divides a polynomial, evaluate the polynomial at the corresponding number: a zero value confirms the factor, and any other value is the remainder. Once one factor is found, dividing it out lowers the degree and the search continues on a smaller polynomial. Repeatedly removing factors eventually leaves an expression that can be factored by the ordinary methods.

## Consequences for graphs

Because a factor corresponds to a zero value, the roots of a polynomial are exactly the places its graph crosses or touches the horizontal axis. A root from a repeated factor makes the graph touch the axis and turn back rather than cross it, which is why multiplicity shows up in the picture. Counting the roots with multiplicity gives the degree, which is the fundamental theorem of algebra read as a statement about factors.
