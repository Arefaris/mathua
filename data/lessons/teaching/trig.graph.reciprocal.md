# Graphs of the Reciprocal Trigonometric Functions

**Reciprocal graphs:** the graphs of cosecant, secant and cotangent, obtained by taking reciprocals of sine, cosine and tangent, with vertical asymptotes wherever the original vanishes.

## Reciprocal shape

The cosecant is one over the sine, so where the sine is large the cosecant is small, and where the sine approaches zero the cosecant grows without bound. The secant is one over the cosine by the same rule, and the cotangent is one over the tangent, or the cosine over the sine. Each reciprocal graph has a vertical asymptote wherever its original is zero, and each passes through the reciprocal of every value the original takes.

## Ranges and asymptotes

The sine and cosine never exceed one in absolute value, so their reciprocals never fall below one in absolute value: the range of the cosecant and of the secant is everything at or beyond one in either direction, with nothing in between. The asymptotes of the cosecant are at whole multiples of 180 degrees, and those of the secant at ninety degrees plus whole multiples of 180. The cotangent has asymptotes where the sine vanishes and zeros where the cosine vanishes.

## Reading a graph back

Given the graph of a sine, the graph of its cosecant is read by inverting the heights: a maximum of one becomes a minimum of one, a value of one half becomes two, and a zero becomes an asymptote. The curves of the reciprocal always meet the original where the original equals one or minus one, because those values are their own reciprocals. This pointwise reading is enough to sketch any reciprocal graph from the graph of its original.
