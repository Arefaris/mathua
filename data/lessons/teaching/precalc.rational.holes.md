# Holes in Rational Graphs

**Hole:** A point where a rational function is undefined because a factor cancelled, but the surrounding values approach a finite limit. The graph has a missing point there rather than an asymptote.

## A cancelled factor is a hole

If the numerator and denominator share the factor $(x-a)$, the function is undefined at $a$, but after cancelling, the simplified function is defined there. The original graph is the simplified graph with the point at $x = a$ removed.

## Worked: (x^2 - 4)/(x - 2)

1. Factor the numerator: $x^2 - 4 = (x-2)(x+2)$.
2. Cancel $x-2$: the simplified function is $x+2$, defined everywhere.
3. The original is undefined at $x = 2$, so the graph is the line $y = x+2$ with a hole at $(2,4)$.

## Holes versus asymptotes

A hole comes from a factor that cancels; a vertical asymptote comes from a factor that remains in the denominator. After cancelling, check which factors are left: the cancelled ones give holes, the surviving ones give asymptotes. A factor that appears more often in the denominator than the numerator still leaves an asymptote after partial cancellation.
