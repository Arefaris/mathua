# The Gamma Function

**Gamma function:** the extension of the factorial to arguments other than the nonnegative integers, defined by an integral and satisfying the recurrence that shifts the argument by one.

## The definition and the recurrence

The gamma function is defined for positive arguments by an integral of the exponential of negative t times t raised to one less than the argument. Integrating by parts once shows that the gamma function of the argument plus one equals the argument times the gamma function of the argument, which is the factorial recurrence. Since the gamma function of one is one, the recurrence gives the gamma function of a positive integer as the factorial of one less than that integer.

## Extending past the integers

The recurrence can be run backwards to define the gamma function at other arguments and to identify its poles. The value at one half is the square root of pi, obtained from the Gaussian integral, and the recurrence then gives the value at every half-integer. The reflection formula connects the values at an argument and at one minus that argument, and the duplication formula relates the value at twice an argument to the values at the argument and the argument plus one half. These identities make the function computable far beyond the integers.

## Why it appears

The gamma function turns up whenever a factorial is generalised: the beta function is a ratio of gamma values, the volume of a ball in many dimensions is a power divided by a gamma value, and the normalisation of the normal and chi-squared distributions uses it. The Cauchy integral formula and the binomial coefficients with a real top also express themselves through it. Reading a factorial-like coefficient as a gamma ratio is the standard way to extend a discrete formula to a continuous parameter.
