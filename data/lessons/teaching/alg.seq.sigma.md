# Sigma Notation

**Sigma notation:** a compact way to write a sum, with an index that runs from a lower limit to an upper limit and a term that depends on the index.

## Reading the notation

The capital sigma stands for a sum. Below it sits the index and its starting value, above it the final value, and to the right the term to be added as the index runs through the integers in between. Expanding the notation is a matter of substituting each integer in turn and adding the results. The index is a dummy: it disappears from the final value, and renaming it changes nothing.

## Sums of simple terms

Two sums recur often enough to be worth memorising: the sum of the first n integers is n times n plus one over two, and the sum of a constant n times is just the constant times n. A sum of a linear term splits into a constant multiple of the first sum plus a constant multiple of the second, using the distributive law across the sum. This turns most textbook sums into a short arithmetic exercise.

## Changing the index

Reindexing shifts where the sum starts and adjusts the term so the total is unchanged, which is useful when a formula assumes the index starts at one. Pulling a constant factor out of the sigma and splitting a sum into two are the other two manipulations worth knowing. Each is justified by the commutativity of addition and leaves the value untouched.
