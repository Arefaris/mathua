# Mass from a variable density

## Density as a function of position

When a rod's density changes along its length, the density is a function of the position rather than a number. A short piece of the rod has a mass approximately its density times its length, with the density read where the piece is.

That local picture is what the integral sums, and it is the reason the answer is an integral rather than a multiplication.

## Integrating along the rod

The mass is the integral of the density over the length, evaluated between the rod's ends. For a density that grows linearly from one end, the integral is a quadratic in the length, so doubling the rod quadruples the mass at a fixed density slope.

The arithmetic is the power rule, and the work is in setting up the integrand rather than in evaluating it.

## Why not multiply

Multiplying an average density by the length gives the same answer here, and the two agree whenever the average is taken correctly. But the average density is only easy when the density is a simple function, and the integral is the method that always works.

Keeping the integral as the definition and the average as a shortcut is what makes the reasoning reusable.
