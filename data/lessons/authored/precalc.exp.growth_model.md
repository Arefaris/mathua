# Exponential growth and decay models

## Repeated multiplication

When a quantity multiplies by the same factor in every period, its size after \(t\) periods is

\[
P(t) = P_0 \cdot a^t.
\]

A culture that doubles every hour is multiplied by \(2^t\) over \(t\) hours, which is why its numbers grow far faster than any repeated addition would give.

## What the base means

A base greater than 1 is growth, and a base between 0 and 1 is decay. A decay of half per period is a base of \(1/2\), and a decay to a tenth is \(1/10\), so the base is the fraction that survives each period rather than the amount lost.

A rate given as a percentage becomes a base of \(1 + r\): growth of 5% per year is a base of 1.05, and decay of 5% per year is 0.95.

## Doubling time and half-life

The time to double solves \(a^t = 2\), so it is \(\log_a 2\), and the half-life is \(\log_a(1/2)\). Neither depends on the starting amount, which is the property that makes them useful: a population's doubling time does not change as the population grows.
