# Reciprocals

## Turning the fraction over

The reciprocal of a fraction is the fraction turned upside down: the numerator and denominator swap places. The reciprocal of two thirds is three halves.

Multiplying a fraction by its reciprocal always gives 1, and that is the property that defines it rather than being a consequence of the swap.

## Why division uses it

Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal, and that rule is the reason reciprocals matter. Instead of memorising a separate division procedure, the division becomes a multiplication the learner already knows.

The rule follows from what division means: asking how many times a fraction fits into something is the same as scaling by its reciprocal.

## Reciprocals of whole numbers

A whole number has a reciprocal too: the reciprocal of 5 is one fifth, because 5 is five over one and turning it over gives one over five. So the reciprocal of a whole number is always a unit fraction.

Zero has no reciprocal, since nothing multiplied by zero gives one. That single exception is why division by zero is undefined rather than merely awkward.
