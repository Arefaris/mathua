# Flux through a surface

## Flux as a flow rate

Flux measures how much of a field crosses a surface. It is largest when the field is perpendicular to the surface and zero when the field is parallel to it, because a field flowing along a surface never crosses it.

For a field of constant strength \(c\) perpendicular to a flat surface of area \(A\), the flux is simply \(cA\).

## A uniform field

The field \((0, 0, 5)\) through a rectangle with sides 3 and 4 lying in the plane \(z = 0\) has flux \(5 \times 12 = 60\) when the surface is oriented upward. Orienting it downward makes the flux \(-60\), because the field then crosses in the negative direction.

For a uniform field only the faces that the field meets contribute, which is why the answer is one product rather than a sum over every face.

## Orientation

A surface has two sides, so a flux needs a choice of which way is positive. For a closed surface the convention is outward, and for an open one it is stated rather than assumed.

Reversing the orientation flips the sign, which is the same rule that reversing a line integral's direction follows.
