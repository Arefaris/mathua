# Tangent lines from a point

## The tangent is perpendicular to the radius

A tangent meets a circle at exactly one point, and the radius drawn to that point is perpendicular to the tangent. That right angle is the fact that makes every tangent problem a right-triangle problem.

A line through a point inside the circle cannot be tangent to it, which is why the point in these problems is always outside.

## Using the right triangle

The radius, the tangent segment and the distance from the external point to the centre form a right triangle with the distance as the hypotenuse. For a radius of 5 and a distance of 13, the tangent length is \(\sqrt{169 - 25} = 12\).

The same triangle answers the converse question: a segment of a given length is tangent exactly when the distance to the centre makes the triangle right.

## Two tangents from one point

The two tangent segments drawn from a single external point are equal in length, because the two right triangles share the hypotenuse and one leg, so they are congruent. That symmetry is used to measure an inaccessible radius and to construct a circle from tangents.

It also means a problem asking for the distance from the point to the point of contact can be answered from either side.
