# Division by zero

Division asks how many times one number fits into another. A zero divisor makes that question impossible to answer with a number.

## What division means

The equation \(12 \div 3 = 4\) means \(3 \times 4 = 12\). In general, \(a \div b = q\) means \(b \times q = a\), with a unique quotient \(q\). This definition is what makes division the inverse of multiplication.

## Why zero cannot be a divisor

Suppose \(5 \div 0 = q\). Then \(0 \times q = 5\). No number times zero equals 5, so no quotient exists. The expression is undefined.

Suppose \(0 \div 0 = q\). Then \(0 \times q = 0\), which is true for every number \(q\). There is no unique quotient, so this expression is also undefined. "Undefined" is stronger than "zero". It means the operation has no value at all.

## Zero divided by a nonzero number

A nonzero divisor is different. For \(0 \div 7\), the equation \(7 \times q = 0\) has exactly one solution, \(q = 0\). So zero divided by any nonzero number is zero.

The two cases are easy to confuse. The position of the zero matters. Zero in the dividend is allowed, and zero in the divisor is not.
