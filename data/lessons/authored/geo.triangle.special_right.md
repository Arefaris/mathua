# The 30-60-90 triangle

## The ratio

A right triangle with a 30 degree angle has its shortest leg opposite that angle, and the ratio of the sides is fixed:

\[
\text{short leg} : \text{long leg} : \text{hypotenuse} = 1 : \sqrt{3} : 2.
\]

So the hypotenuse is twice the shortest leg. A short leg of 7 gives a hypotenuse of 14 and a long leg of \(7\sqrt{3}\), which is irrational and rarely asked for in a whole-number problem.

## From the hypotenuse back

The relation works in both directions: a hypotenuse of 18 has a shortest leg of 9. Dividing the hypotenuse by 2 is the shorter half of the ratio, and it is exact whenever the hypotenuse is even, which is why the values are chosen that way.

The long leg is the one that introduces the square root, so problems that want a whole answer ask about the other two sides.

## Where the ratio comes from

Taking an equilateral triangle and dropping a perpendicular from one vertex cuts it into two congruent right triangles with angles of 30, 60 and 90 degrees. The perpendicular bisects the base, which gives the short leg as half a side, and the original side is the hypotenuse, which gives the 2 in the ratio; the square root of 3 comes from Pythagoras applied to those two.

The 45-45-90 triangle has a similar fixed ratio, \(1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}\), and both are worth recognising because they turn a trigonometric question into arithmetic.
