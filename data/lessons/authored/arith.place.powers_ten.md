# Multiplying and dividing by powers of ten

Multiplying or dividing by 10, 100 or 1000 moves every digit to a new place. The digits themselves do not change.

## The place-value shift

A power of ten is a one followed by zeros: 10, 100, 1000. Each zero corresponds to one place. Multiplying by 10 moves a digit one place to the left, so its value becomes ten times larger. Multiplying by 100 moves it two places, and so on.

Dividing reverses the move. Dividing by 10 moves every digit one place to the right, making its value ten times smaller.

## Multiplying

For \(37 \times 100\), the factor 100 has two zeros, so each digit of 37 moves two places left. The 3 tens become 3 thousands, and the 7 ones become 7 hundreds:

\[
37 \times 100 = 3700.
\]

When zeros are available, appending them is the same shift. For 37, appending two zeros gives 3700.

## Dividing

For \(4200 \div 100\), the divisor has two zeros, so each digit moves two places right. The 4 thousands become 4 tens, and the 2 hundreds become 2 ones:

\[
4200 \div 100 = 42.
\]

Check a division by multiplying the result by the same power: \(42 \times 100 = 4200\). That check catches a shift in the wrong direction.
