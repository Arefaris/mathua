# End behavior of a polynomial

Far from the origin, a polynomial behaves like its leading term. The other terms change the shape near the origin, but they cannot compete with the highest power once \(x\) is large in either direction.

## Degree decides the ends

An even degree sends both ends in the same direction. An odd degree sends them in opposite directions, because a negative number raised to an odd power stays negative.

This is why a parabola has two arms pointing the same way and a cubic has one arm up and one down. The degree alone fixes which of the two shapes applies.

## The leading sign flips them

The leading coefficient decides whether the common direction is up or down. If the degree is even and the coefficient is positive, both ends go up; a negative coefficient sends both ends down.

For an odd degree, a positive coefficient sends the left end down and the right end up. A negative coefficient reverses that. The right end always follows the sign of the leading coefficient, because a large positive \(x\) raised to any power is positive.

## The four cases

There are only four answers:

- even degree, positive leading coefficient: up on both ends;
- even degree, negative leading coefficient: down on both ends;
- odd degree, positive leading coefficient: down on the left, up on the right;
- odd degree, negative leading coefficient: up on the left, down on the right.

Classify the degree as even or odd, then read the sign. The lower-order terms are irrelevant to the answer, which is why the question can give a complicated polynomial and still have a short answer.
