# Multi-step word problems

Some problems cannot be answered with one operation. Read for the quantity that must be built first, then use it in the next step.

## Find the first quantity

Look for a group and a size per group. "Six boxes with twelve pencils each" describes six equal groups. The first step multiplies:

\[
6 \times 12 = 72.
\]

Name the quantity and its unit. Here the first quantity is 72 pencils, not just 72.

## Use it in the next step

The problem then changes that quantity. "It sells 35 pencils" means subtract 35 from the 72 pencils:

\[
72 - 35 = 37.
\]

Keep the units attached to each number. Adding pencils to boxes, or subtracting a count of boxes from a count of pencils, is the usual sign that the wrong quantity was used.

## Answer the question asked

Read the final sentence again. It may ask for what remains, how many groups are needed, or how many more are required. In the pencil problem it asks how many pencils are left, so the answer is 37 pencils.

A two-step problem can be checked by working backward. Add the amount sold back to the answer. The result should recover the first quantity: \(37 + 35 = 72\), and \(72 \div 6 = 12\) pencils per box.
