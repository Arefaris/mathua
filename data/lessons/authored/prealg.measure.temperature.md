# Celsius and Fahrenheit

Celsius and Fahrenheit are two temperature scales with different zero points and different step sizes. A temperature can be converted from one scale to the other with a formula.

## Use the conversion formula

To convert Celsius to Fahrenheit:

\[
F = \frac{9C}{5} + 32.
\]

To convert Fahrenheit to Celsius:

\[
C = \frac{5(F - 32)}{9}.
\]

The two formulas are inverses, so converting a temperature and then converting back returns the original value.

## Substitute before computing

For \(20^\circ\text{C}\),

\[
F = \frac{9(20)}{5} + 32 = 36 + 32 = 68.
\]

So \(20^\circ\text{C} = 68^\circ\text{F}\). For \(68^\circ\text{F}\),

\[
C = \frac{5(68 - 32)}{9} = \frac{5(36)}{9} = 20.
\]

Keep the parentheses around the subtraction in the Fahrenheit-to-Celsius formula.

## Check the reference points

Water freezes at \(0^\circ\text{C} = 32^\circ\text{F}\) and boils at \(100^\circ\text{C} = 212^\circ\text{F}\). A result outside the range of the inputs can still be correct, because the scales have different zero points. Use a known reference point to check whether the conversion moved in the right direction.
