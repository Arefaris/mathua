# Extrema on a closed region

## Interior and boundary

A maximum on a closed region can happen in two places: at an interior critical point, or somewhere on the boundary. Both have to be checked, and the boundary is where the work usually is.

For a function that decreases away from the origin over a disc, the interior point at the centre wins and the boundary can be dismissed by the shape of the function rather than computed.

## The closed-region theorem

A continuous function on a closed and bounded region always attains a maximum and a minimum. That is the extreme value theorem, and it is what guarantees the search will succeed: for an open region the same function might approach a maximum without reaching it.

The theorem says a maximum exists, not where it is, so it is a licence to look rather than a method.

## Checking both

The method is to find the interior critical points, then restrict the function to the boundary and find its extrema there, and finally compare the values. On a circular boundary the restriction usually becomes a one-variable problem.

For a bowl whose only critical point is at the centre and whose values fall as the variables grow, the boundary step can be skipped, but the reason should be stated rather than assumed.
