# Commission

A commission is a payment based on a percentage of sales. It is a percent-of-a-number problem with a workplace context: the sales amount is the whole, and the commission rate is the percent.

## Commission is a percent of sales

The commission equals the rate multiplied by the sales amount:

\[
\text{commission} = \text{rate} \times \text{sales}.
\]

A salesperson who earns \(6\%\) on \(\$1{,}200\) in sales earns a commission on the full \(\$1{,}200\), not on the number of items sold.

## Convert the rate to a decimal

Write the percent as a decimal before multiplying. \(6\% = 0.06\), so

\[
0.06 \times 1200 = 72.
\]

The commission is \(\$72\). Multiplying by 6 instead of 0.06 gives \(\$7{,}200\), which is larger than the sales and cannot be a commission below \(100\%\).

## Check the amount

A commission is part of the sales, so it must be less than the sales when the rate is below \(100\%\). Keep the decimal point aligned with the money: \(\$72.00\), not \(\$720.00\). If the rate is \(6\%\), six cents of every dollar of sales goes to the salesperson.
