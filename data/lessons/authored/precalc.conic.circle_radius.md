# The circle in general form

## Completing the square

A circle written as \(x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0\) does not show its centre or its radius, and completing the square in \(x\) and in \(y\) puts it in the form

\[
(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2,
\]

where the centre is \((h,k)\). The centre is \((-D/2, -E/2)\) and \(r^2 = (D/2)^2 + (E/2)^2 - F\).

## Reading the radius

For \(x^2 + y^2 + 6x + 8y + 9 = 0\) the centre is \((-3,-4)\) and \(r^2 = 9 + 16 - 9 = 16\), so the radius is 4.

The right-hand side has to be positive for the equation to describe a circle: zero gives a single point, and a negative value gives nothing at all in the real plane.

## From the general form to the standard one

Every conic has a general form and a standard one, and completing the square converts between them. For an ellipse or a hyperbola the same step is applied in both variables after the coefficients are normalised, which is why the coefficients matter and not only the constants.

For a circle the \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) coefficients are equal, and that equality is what distinguishes a circle from the other conics when they are written in general form.
