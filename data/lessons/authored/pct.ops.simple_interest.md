# Simple interest

Simple interest is a charge for borrowing money or a payment for saving money. It is calculated on the original principal only, so the interest does not compound.

## The simple-interest formula

The formula is

\[
I = P \times r \times t,
\]

where \(P\) is the principal, \(r\) is the annual interest rate written as a decimal, and \(t\) is the time in years. The result \(I\) is the interest, not the total balance.

## Convert the rate and keep time in years

Write the percent as a decimal before multiplying. \(5\% = 0.05\). If the time is given in months, convert it to years first: 6 months is 0.5 year, and 18 months is 1.5 years.

For \(\$1{,}200\) at \(5\%\) for 3 years,

\[
I = 1200 \times 0.05 \times 3 = 180.
\]

The interest is \(\$180\).

## Interest is not the balance

The interest is the extra amount. The total amount owed or available is principal plus interest: \(1200 + 180 = 1380\). Simple interest uses the original principal every year; it does not add the first year's interest to the principal before calculating the second year's interest.
