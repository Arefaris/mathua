# Midsegments

## Half of the third side

A midsegment joins the midpoints of two sides of a triangle. It is parallel to the third side and half its length, so a third side of 14 gives a midsegment of 7.

Those two properties together are the whole theorem, and the parallelism is what makes the midsegment useful for bisecting a field or finding a distance that cannot be measured directly.

## Why it is half

The midsegment cuts off a smaller triangle at the vertex, and that triangle is similar to the whole one with a ratio of one half: both of the sides it uses are half their originals, and the angle between them is shared.

Similarity then gives the third side as half as well, and the corresponding angles are equal, which is what makes the two lines parallel.

## Three midsegments

Drawing all three midsegments cuts a triangle into four congruent smaller triangles, each similar to the original with ratio one half. That subdivision is why the midsegment is the natural unit for a first similarity argument.

It also shows that the middle triangle is the same shape as the original and one quarter of its area, since area scales by the square of the ratio.
