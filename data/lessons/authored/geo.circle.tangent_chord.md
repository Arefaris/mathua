# The tangent and a chord

## The tangent-chord angle

An angle formed by a tangent and a chord drawn from the point of contact is half the arc the chord cuts off. An intercepted arc of 100 degrees gives an angle of 50.

The angle is measured at the point of contact and opens into the circle, and the arc it looks at is the one on the far side of the chord.

## Half the intercepted arc

The result looks like the inscribed-angle theorem, and it is: the tangent can be viewed as the limit of a chord whose far endpoint slides around the circle, and in the limit the inscribed angle becomes the tangent-chord angle while the arc stays the same.

That limiting argument is why the factor of one half survives, and why the two theorems are usually taught together.

## The same halving as before

Every angle in the circle theorems is half of an arc, and the variety comes from where the vertex sits. On the circle gives an inscribed angle, at the centre gives a central angle of the full arc, and on a tangent gives this one.

Choosing the right one is a matter of locating the vertex, so it is worth naming where each angle's vertex is before computing anything.
