# Loci in the complex plane

A condition on \(z\) selects a set of points, and the usual conditions are written with moduli: a fixed distance from one point gives a circle, and equal distances to two points give a line.

## A fixed distance from a point

The equation \(|z - z_0| = r\) says that the distance from \(z\) to \(z_0\) is \(r\). The set is the circle centred at \(z_0\) with radius \(r\), and it is a circle rather than a disc because the equality excludes both the interior and the exterior.

For \(|z - (2+3i)| = 4\) the centre is \(2 + 3i\) and the radius is four. The point \(2 + 3i\) is not a solution, its distance to itself is zero.

## Equal distances to two points

The equation \(|z - z_1| = |z - z_2|\) says that \(z\) is as far from \(z_1\) as from \(z_2\). The set is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining them, because a point is equidistant from two points exactly when it lies on that perpendicular line.

For \(|z - 1| = |z - 5|\) the solution is the line \(\operatorname{Re} z = 3\): every point with real part three is equally far from one and five.

## Reading a description back

Going the other way, "the circle centred at \(i\) with radius two" is \(|z - i| = 2\), and "the perpendicular bisector of 0 and \(4i\)" is \(|z| = |z - 4i|\). Translating in both directions is the skill.

The general condition \(|z - z_1| = k|z - z_2|\) for \(k \neq 1\) gives a circle rather than a line, and the line is the limiting case \(k = 1\).
