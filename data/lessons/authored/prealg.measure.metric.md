# Metric unit conversion

Metric units are built from prefixes that are powers of ten. Converting between them moves the decimal point instead of changing the digits.

## Read the prefix

The prefix tells you the size of the unit relative to the base unit:

- kilo- means 1000;
- centi- means \(\frac{1}{100}\);
- milli- means \(\frac{1}{1000}\).

So 1 kilometre is 1000 metres, 1 metre is 100 centimetres, and 1 centimetre is 10 millimetres.

## Move the decimal point

Converting between metric units moves the decimal point by the number of places in the power of ten. Kilometres to metres moves it 3 places to the right, because the unit is getting smaller. For 2.5 kilometres,

\[
2.5 \text{ km} = 2500 \text{ m}.
\]

Metres to centimetres moves it 2 places to the right; centimetres to millimetres moves it 1 place.

## Check the direction

A larger unit gives a smaller number, and a smaller unit gives a larger number. 2500 metres is the same length as 2.5 kilometres. The number grew because the unit shrank; the measurement itself did not change.
