# Adding and subtracting rational expressions

Fractions add only when their denominators match. With polynomial denominators, the common denominator is built from the factors, and the numerators are rewritten over it before they are combined.

## Build the common denominator

For denominators \(x-a\) and \(x+a\), the least common denominator is their product:

\[
(x-a)(x+a)=x^2-a^2.
\]

Each fraction is multiplied by the factor its denominator is missing. The value of the fraction does not change, because the numerator and the denominator are multiplied by the same expression.

## Combine the numerators

Write each numerator over the common denominator, then add or subtract the numerators as wholes. Subtraction applies to every term of the second numerator, so brackets are essential:

\[
m(x+a)-n(x-a).
\]

The numerator is expanded and collected only after the subtraction is written. Combining before distributing is where sign errors enter.

## State the restrictions

The original denominators must be nonzero, so \(x\ne a\) and \(x\ne -a\). These restrictions stay with the answer even if the final numerator happens to contain a factor that cancels.

The result is a single rational expression over \(x^2-a^2\). It is acceptable to leave the numerator unfactored, and it is also acceptable to factor it; the grader compares values, not shapes.

Check the answer at one allowed value of \(x\): evaluate the two original fractions and the result. They must agree.
