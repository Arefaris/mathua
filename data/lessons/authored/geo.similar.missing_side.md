# Finding a missing side by proportionality

## Setting up the proportion

A missing side in similar figures is found by writing the ratios of corresponding sides as a proportion and solving it. If two triangles are similar and the sides of the smaller are 3 and 4 while the corresponding side of the larger is 9, the proportion is

\[
\frac{9}{3} = \frac{x}{4},
\]

so \(x = 12\). The set-up is the work; the arithmetic is one multiplication.

## Solving for the unknown

Cross-multiplication turns the proportion into a linear equation. Writing the proportion as two equal fractions and multiplying both sides by the product of the denominators gives the unknown directly.

The answer should be checked for plausibility: if the factor is greater than 1 the unknown side must be *larger* than its counterpart, and an answer smaller than the side it corresponds to means the proportion was written upside down.

## Choosing the corresponding sides

Sides correspond when they lie between the same two angles, which the angle markings or the order of the vertices in the names identifies. Writing the similarity statement with the vertices in corresponding order — \(ABC\) similar to \(DEF\) — makes the pairing unambiguous, which is why that order is part of the notation rather than decoration.
