# Distance between two points

## The distance formula

The distance between two points is

\[
d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}.
\]

The two differences are the legs of a right triangle whose hypotenuse joins the points, and the formula is the Pythagorean theorem applied to that triangle.

For \((1,2)\) and \((4,6)\) the differences are 3 and 4, so the distance is \(\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5\).

## It is the Pythagorean theorem

Reading the formula as a theorem rather than as something to memorise is what makes it usable in three dimensions: adding a third squared difference gives the distance in space, and the same argument with one more leg applies.

The order of subtraction does not matter, because each difference is squared, which is why the formula is symmetric in the two points.

## Midpoints and the same idea

The midpoint of a segment is the average of the endpoints, \(\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)\), which is the same "add the coordinates" idea as the distance formula's differences, applied by averaging instead of subtracting.

Both are the plane's arithmetic: points are added and scaled coordinate by coordinate, and the distance formula is the length of a difference.
