# The least common denominator

## Finding the smallest common multiple

The least common denominator of two fractions is the least common multiple of their denominators. For denominators of 4 and 6 it is 12, since 12 is the first number both divide.

Any common multiple works for the addition, but the smallest keeps the numerators small and the reducing short.

## Multiplying is not the same as least

Multiplying the two denominators always gives a common multiple, since each denominator divides its own product with the other. It is rarely the least one: for 4 and 6 the product is 24 and the least is 12.

Using the product is not wrong, only wasteful, and the answer reduces to the same fraction either way. Knowing the difference is what lets a learner choose the faster route on larger numbers.

## Why it matters for adding

Adding fractions requires a common denominator, and finding the least one is the step that decides how much work the rest is. That is why the least common multiple is introduced beside fraction addition rather than as a number-theory fact on its own.

When one denominator divides the other, the larger is already the least common denominator, and that case is worth recognising before doing any factoring.
