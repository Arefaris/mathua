# Equilibrium points

## Where the rate is zero

An equilibrium of an autonomous equation is a value where the rate of change is zero, so a solution starting there stays there. Finding them means solving the right-hand side for zero, not integrating anything.

The equation's right-hand side depends only on the state, which is what makes this a question about values rather than about functions.

## Reading the roots off the factors

When the right-hand side is given as a product of factors, each factor being zero gives an equilibrium. A logistic model written as a rate times the state times the remaining capacity therefore has one equilibrium at zero and one at the capacity.

Factored forms are the ones to prefer, because the equilibria can be read rather than solved for.

## Which equilibrium is interesting

Zero usually means extinction or absence, and the non-zero one is the state the system tends toward. Which is stable depends on the sign of the rate on either side, and that is a separate question from where the equilibria are.

The non-zero equilibrium is usually the answer wanted, and it is the one that carries the model's meaning.
