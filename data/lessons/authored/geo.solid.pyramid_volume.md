# The volume of a pyramid

## One third of a prism

A pyramid's volume is one third of the prism with the same base and height:

\[
V = \frac{1}{3}\,B\,h
\]

where \(B\) is the base area. A pyramid with a base of area 24 and a height of 5 has volume 40.

The same formula covers every pyramid, whatever the shape of its base, so the base area is the only part that depends on the base's form.

## Why the third

Three congruent pyramids can be assembled into the prism, which shows where the third comes from for the square-based case. The general proof slices the pyramid into thin slabs and compares each slab with the prism's, and the same third emerges.

Accepting the third as a fact to verify in the simple case is what a first course does; the slicing argument is what makes it general.

## Cones and other pyramids

A cone is a pyramid with a circular base, so its volume is one third of the cylinder's: the base area is the circle's and the formula is otherwise identical. That is why the third, the base area and the height are the three things to identify in any of these problems.

A shape that is a prism with a pyramid on top is handled by adding the two volumes, and the parts are computed with the two different formulas rather than one.
