# Scaling a recipe

## Amount per serving

A recipe gives an amount for a number of servings, so the amount for one serving is that amount divided by the serving count. That division is the step that makes the recipe scalable at all.

From there any number of servings is a multiplication, and the two steps together are one proportion.

## Multiplying back up

To serve a different number, multiply the per-serving amount by the new count. The arithmetic is a fraction multiplication and the result usually reduces.

Reading the whole calculation as "find one, then find many" is what keeps the two steps in the right order and prevents the common mistake of multiplying by the wrong ratio.

## Keeping the units

The answer is an amount of an ingredient, so it carries the same unit as the recipe gave. Keeping the unit attached is what makes the answer checkable in a kitchen, and it catches the error of scaling in the wrong direction: more servings must need more of the ingredient, never less.

A quick check against the original amount catches the rest.
