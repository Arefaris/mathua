# Translating words into expressions

An algebraic expression uses numbers, variables, and operations to describe a quantity. Translating a phrase into an expression means matching each word or group of words to an operation.

## Identify the operations

Common signals are:

- "more than" or "the sum" for addition;
- "less than" or "the difference" for subtraction;
- "times", "product", or "of" for multiplication;
- "quotient" or "divided by" for division.

The variable \(x\) stands for the unknown number. "Five more than three times a number" becomes \(3x + 5\).

## Keep the order of subtraction and division

Subtraction and division are not commutative, so the order of the words matters. "Four less than the product of six and \(x\)" means \(6x - 4\), not \(4 - 6x\). The phrase "less than" reverses the order: the product comes first, and 4 is subtracted from it.

"The quotient of \(x\) and 5" means \(\frac{x}{5}\), not \(\frac{5}{x}\). The number named first is the numerator or the dividend.

## Use grouping for a difference or sum

When a phrase describes a difference or a sum and then multiplies the result, use parentheses. "Eight times the difference of \(x\) and 2" means \(8(x - 2)\), because the difference is calculated first.

Test the translation with a value. If \(x = 5\), the difference is 3, and eight times it is 24. The expression \(8x - 2\) would give 38, so it is not the same phrase.
