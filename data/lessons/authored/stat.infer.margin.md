# The margin of error

## Half the interval's width

The margin of error is the critical value multiplied by the standard error, and it is half the width of the confidence interval. An interval is reported as the estimate plus or minus the margin, so the margin is the part that expresses uncertainty.

A larger margin means a less precise estimate, and it comes from a larger critical value or a larger standard error.

## The critical value

The critical value is chosen from the confidence level: about 2 for 95 percent and about 3 for 99 percent, with the exact values coming from the normal or t distribution. Choosing it is the step that decides the level of confidence the interval claims.

A higher confidence level needs a wider interval, so there is a trade between the certainty claimed and the precision offered.

## What it does not say

The margin of error assumes the sample was drawn without bias, and it says nothing about a sample that was not. A confidence interval from a biased sample is a precise answer to the wrong question, and no margin can repair it.

That is why a reported interval is always worth reading together with how the data was collected, and why the number alone is not the finding.
