# The fraction remaining

## The whole is one

The remaining part of a whole is found by subtracting what is used from one, written over the same denominator. A tank that is three quarters full is one quarter empty, and the subtraction is the whole calculation.

Writing the whole as a fraction over the denominator is the step that makes the subtraction possible, and it is the same conversion a whole-number subtraction needs.

## Reading it as a remainder

The answer is a fraction of the whole rather than a count, so it is comparable to the part used and the two together make one. That check is worth doing: the used and remaining fractions must sum to a whole.

A negative answer means the parts were swapped, and an answer larger than the used part means the subtraction was of the wrong quantity.

## Why the wording matters

Questions about what is left, what remains and how much is empty are all the same computation, and recognising the wording is what turns a word problem into the arithmetic. That recognition is the whole difficulty of the applied problems, not the subtraction itself.

The same reading applies to a fraction of a group that has been partly used, which is why this concept sits beside the fraction-of-a-number one.
