# Unit price and the better buy

A larger package is not automatically a better deal. To compare two packages fairly, compare their prices for the same amount of product. That price is the unit price.

## Find each unit price

Divide the price by the number of units:

\[
\text{unit price} = \frac{\text{price}}{\text{quantity}}.
\]

Use the same unit for both packages. If one package is measured in ounces and the other in pounds, convert one first. For 12 ounces at \(\$3.60\),

\[
\frac{3.60}{12} = 0.30.
\]

The unit price is \(\$0.30\) per ounce.

## Compare the unit prices

The lower unit price is the better buy. For a second package of 16 ounces at \(\$4.00\),

\[
\frac{4.00}{16} = 0.25.
\]

The second package costs \(\$0.25\) per ounce, so it is the better buy even though its total price is higher.

## Check the comparison

Multiply the unit price back by the quantity to recover the total price. For the 16-ounce package, \(16 \times 0.25 = 4.00\). Comparing total prices alone would pick the cheaper package, but comparing unit prices answers the real question: which package gives more product for each dollar?
