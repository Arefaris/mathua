# Classifying a conic

A conic section is a circle, an ellipse, a parabola or a hyperbola. Its equation, before any rotation, can be classified by looking at which variables are squared and how their coefficients compare.

## Count the squared terms

A parabola has exactly one squared variable. An equation such as \(y=ax^2+bx+c\) is a parabola because \(y\) is not squared.

The other three conics have both \(x^2\) and \(y^2\). That single observation separates the parabola from the rest before any coefficient is read.

## Compare the coefficients

When both variables are squared with the same sign, the coefficients decide between a circle and an ellipse. Equal coefficients give a circle; unequal coefficients give an ellipse.

An equation such as \(x^2+y^2=25\) has equal coefficients of one, so it is a circle. Writing the same circle as \(\frac{x^2}{25}+\frac{y^2}{25}=1\) keeps the coefficients equal, and it is still a circle, not an ellipse.

## Check the signs

Opposite signs between the squared terms give a hyperbola. The equation \(\frac{x^2}{9}-\frac{y^2}{4}=1\) has a plus for \(x^2\) and a minus for \(y^2\), so it is a hyperbola.

The four cases are therefore:

- one squared variable: parabola;
- two squared variables, equal coefficients: circle;
- two squared variables, unequal coefficients, same sign: ellipse;
- two squared variables, opposite signs: hyperbola.

Read the equation in that order. The classification does not require completing the square or finding a center.
