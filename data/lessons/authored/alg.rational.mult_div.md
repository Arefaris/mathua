# Multiplying and dividing rational expressions

Multiplication of rational expressions is direct: multiply numerators and multiply denominators, then cancel. Division adds one step, because dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal.

## Multiply across

For two fractions,

\[
\frac{A}{B}\cdot\frac{C}{D}=\frac{AC}{BD}.
\]

Factor before multiplying. Multiplying out first produces a larger polynomial and hides the common factors, while a factored product shows what can be cancelled immediately.

## Invert for division

Division by a fraction is multiplication by its reciprocal:

\[
\frac{A}{B}\div\frac{C}{D}=\frac{A}{B}\cdot\frac{D}{C}.
\]

Invert the second fraction only. Inverting the first as well is the commonest error, and it changes the answer even when the factors look symmetric.

## Cancel and state the restrictions

Every denominator in the original problem must be nonzero, and so must the numerator of the divisor. A factor that was cancelled still contributes its excluded value.

Collect the excluded values before writing the simplified answer. If the original expression required \(x\ne -3\) and \(x\ne 2\), the answer requires both, even when neither factor appears in the final expression.

The simplified result is checked by evaluating the original and the answer at a few allowed values. They must agree wherever both are defined.
