# The volume of a prism

## Base area times height

A prism has two congruent parallel bases and every cross-section parallel to them is the same region, so its volume is the base area times the height:

\[
V = B\,h.
\]

A prism with a base of area 24 and a height of 7 has volume 168, whatever shape that base is.

## Any base shape

The formula does not care whether the base is a triangle, a hexagon or a circle, which is why the base's *area* is what the problem supplies rather than its dimensions. That is also what makes it the comparison figure for a pyramid or a cone, whose volume is a third of it.

The distinction from a pyramid is worth stating: a prism has the same cross-section all the way up, and a pyramid's shrinks to a point, so the average cross-section is a third of the base rather than all of it.

## Composite solids

A solid made of a prism and a pyramid on top is handled by computing the two volumes with their own formulas and adding them. Splitting the solid at the join is the step that makes it two problems rather than a harder one.

For a solid that is a prism with a cylindrical hole, the volume is the outer prism minus the cylinder, which is the same idea read as a subtraction.
