# Quadratic word problems about area

An area problem becomes quadratic when a rectangle's length is described in terms of its width. The equation comes from multiplying two expressions for the sides, and the geometry decides which root is allowed.

## Name the width

Let \(w\) be the width in meters. Translate the sentence about the length into an expression in \(w\): a length that is some meters more than a multiple of the width has the form \(mw+d\).

The area is the product of the two sides, so

\[
w(mw+d)=A.
\]

Naming the variable is not decoration. It fixes what the answer means and prevents the common mistake of solving for the length when the question asks for the width.

## Build the equation

Expand the product and move the area to the same side:

\[
mw^2+dw-A=0.
\]

This is a quadratic in \(w\). The coefficients are small integers because the problem was built from integer sides, so factoring is often faster than the formula. Both roots solve the equation, but only one describes a rectangle.

## Reject the negative root

A width cannot be negative. The product of the roots is negative because the constant term \(-A\) is negative, so the roots have opposite signs: precisely one is positive.

That positive root is the width. The negative root is a solution of the algebra but not of the geometry, and the explanation should say so rather than leave it unexplained.

Check the answer by rebuilding the length and multiplying: the area must be the number stated in the question.
