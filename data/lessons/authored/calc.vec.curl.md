# The curl of a planar field

## The cross-partial difference

For a planar field with components \(P\) and \(Q\), the scalar curl is

\[\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}.\]

It is a single number at each point in the plane, unlike the vector curl of a field in space, because a plane has only one axis about which a rotation can turn.

## What non-zero curl means

A field with zero curl everywhere on a simply connected region is the gradient of some function. A field with non-zero curl cannot be one, and the value measures how strongly the field turns: for \(\mathbf{F} = (y, x)\) the curl is \(1 - 1 = 0\), a shear that does not turn, while for \(\mathbf{F} = (-y, x)\) the curl is \(1 - (-1) = 2\), the field that circles the origin.

## Curl and circulation

The curl is a density of circulation. Integrating it over a region gives the circulation of the field around that region's boundary, which is Green's theorem, and it is why a small paddle wheel placed in a field with non-zero curl spins.
