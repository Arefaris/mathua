# Adding three fractions

## The same rule twice

Three fractions are added by putting all of them over a common denominator and then adding the numerators. Nothing new is needed: the rule for two fractions applies to the first pair and then to the result.

A common denominator for three fractions is any number all three denominators divide, and the smallest such number keeps the arithmetic light.

## Keeping the denominator in view

The numerator work is the part that changes and the denominator is the part that must not. Writing the common denominator once and converting each fraction to it is what keeps the three terms comparable.

Adding numerators while the denominators differ is the error to watch for, and it produces an answer that is smaller than the true sum rather than merely simplified.

## Reducing at the end

The sum is usually not in lowest terms, since a common denominator is rarely the smallest one for all three. Reducing at the end is one step and safer than trying to cancel during the addition.

Estimating first is a good check: if the three fractions are each near a half, the sum should be near one and a half, and an answer far from that means an arithmetic slip rather than a simplification one.
