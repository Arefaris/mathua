# Fractions of a unit of measure

## Parts of a unit

A physical unit divides into a fixed number of parts, and some number of those parts is a fraction of the unit. Forty-five minutes is forty-five sixtieths of an hour, which reduces to three quarters.

The part count is the denominator and the parts taken are the numerator, which is the same reading as a fraction of any other whole.

## Reducing the fraction

The fraction usually reduces, and it should: three quarters of an hour is easier to read than forty-five sixtieths. Reducing for a unit whose part count is not a power of ten is where the skill earns its place, since the answer is not automatically in decimal form.

Units that divide into 100 parts give a fraction that turns into a decimal directly, which is why money and centimetres are friendly and time is not.

## Units that are not decimal

An hour has sixty minutes and a day twenty-four hours, so fractions of them reduce to denominators that have nothing to do with tens. That is why fractions remain useful after decimals have been introduced: the unit's own structure decides the denominator, not the counting system.

Knowing the conversion factor is the first step of any such problem, and the fraction is the second.
