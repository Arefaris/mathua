# Hyperbolic functions on imaginary arguments

The hyperbolic functions are defined by \(\cosh x = \frac{e^x + e^{-x}}{2}\) and \(\sinh x = \frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{2}\). Substituting \(x = i\theta\) turns them into the trigonometric ones, with one factor of \(i\) in the sine case.

## Cosh of an imaginary angle

\(\cosh(i\theta) = \frac{e^{i\theta} + e^{-i\theta}}{2}\), which is exactly the exponential identity for \(\cos\theta\). So \(\cosh(i\theta) = \cos\theta\) for real \(\theta\), with no factor of \(i\) anywhere.

## Sinh of an imaginary angle

\(\sinh(i\theta) = \frac{e^{i\theta} - e^{-i\theta}}{2} = i\sin\theta\), using \(e^{i\theta} - e^{-i\theta} = 2i\sin\theta\). The \(i\) survives because the denominator is 2 rather than \(2i\).

So the pair is \(\cosh(i\theta) = \cos\theta\) and \(\sinh(i\theta) = i\sin\theta\). Mixing them up is the usual error, and the missing \(i\) is the tell.

## The rotation reading

The identities say that a hyperbolic rotation through an imaginary angle is an ordinary rotation. That is why the two families have parallel addition formulas and why \(\cosh^2 - \sinh^2 = 1\) becomes \(\cos^2 + \sin^2 = 1\) on the imaginary axis.

For a real angle the hyperbolic functions describe curves that open without repeating; for an imaginary angle they become the periodic trigonometric ones.
