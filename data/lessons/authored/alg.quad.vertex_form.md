# Vertex form by completing the square

Vertex form writes a quadratic as \(y=(x-h)^2+k\), which displays the vertex \((h,k)\) directly. Converting from \(y=x^2+bx+c\) is completing the square, and it is a rewriting rather than a new equation.

## Complete the square

Take half of the \(x\)-coefficient and square it. For \(x^2+bx\),

\[
x^2+bx=\left(x+\frac{b}{2}\right)^2-\frac{b^2}{4}.
\]

Adding the square and subtracting it changes nothing, so the expression keeps its value. With the quadratic monic, the coefficient of \(x^2\) is one, and the completed square always has the form \((x-h)^2\).

## Read off the vertex

The value inside the square is \(h=-\frac{b}{2}\). The constant left outside is \(k=c-\frac{b^2}{4}\). In symbols,

\[
x^2+bx+c=(x-h)^2+k.
\]

The vertex is \((h,k)\), and because the leading coefficient is positive it is the minimum. The axis of symmetry is the vertical line \(x=h\), which is a useful check on the sign of \(h\).

## Check by expanding

Expand \((x-h)^2+k\) and compare coefficients. The \(x\)-coefficient must be \(-2h\), and the constant must be \(h^2+k\). If either fails, the sign of \(h\) or the adjustment to \(k\) is wrong.

The pair is entered as \(h\) first and \(k\) second, in that order, separated by a comma. The vertex itself is a point, but the question asks for the two numbers.
