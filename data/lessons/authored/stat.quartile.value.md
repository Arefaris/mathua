# Quartiles

## Quartiles as medians of halves

The quartiles cut a sorted data set into four groups of about equal size. The lower quartile is the median of the lower half and the upper quartile the median of the upper half, so finding them is finding two medians rather than dividing the range.

The middle quartile is the median itself, which is why only two of the three quartiles are usually reported.

## Five-number summaries

The minimum, the lower quartile, the median, the upper quartile and the maximum together are the five-number summary, and they are what a box plot draws. The two quartiles give the box's edges and the median its line, with the extremes as the whiskers.

The summary describes a distribution's shape coarsely but honestly: where its middle sits, how wide that middle is, and how far the extremes reach.

## Reading them from a sorted list

The data must be sorted first, and the halves are split at the median. With an even count the halves are equal; with an odd count the median itself is excluded from both halves, which is the convention that keeps the quartiles symmetric.

Counting from the ends is a useful check: the lower quartile should have about a quarter of the values below it, and the upper quartile about three quarters.
