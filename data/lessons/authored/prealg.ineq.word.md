# Inequality word problems

An inequality word problem describes a range of allowed values rather than one exact value. The words "at most" and "at least" decide the direction of the inequality.

## Choose the inequality symbol

"At most" means the quantity cannot exceed a limit, so use \(\le\). "At least" means the quantity must reach a minimum, so use \(\ge\). "No more than" is another way to say "at most", and "no less than" is another way to say "at least".

## Write the inequality

Let \(x\) be the unknown number of items, miles, hours, or people. A fixed amount plus a repeated amount has the form

\[
\text{rate} \cdot x + \text{fixed}.
\]

Compare that expression with the limit. A taxi that charges \(\$5\) plus \(\$3\) per mile, with at most \(\$17\) to spend, gives

\[
3x + 5 \le 17.
\]

## Solve and interpret the whole-number answer

Subtract the fixed amount from both sides:

\[
3x \le 12.
\]

Divide by 3:

\[
x \le 4.
\]

The greatest whole number of miles is 4. When the question asks for the least whole number and the inequality points to the right, round up to the first whole number that satisfies it. Always return to the story to check that a fractional answer makes sense.
