# Comparing to one half

## Comparing without a common denominator

One half is the benchmark that makes comparison cheap. A fraction is greater than a half exactly when twice its numerator is greater than its denominator, so the comparison is between two whole numbers and needs no rewriting.

For three quarters, twice the numerator is six and the denominator is four, so the fraction is above a half.

## Why twice the numerator

Comparing a fraction to one half is the same as asking whether the fraction exceeds the number with numerator one and denominator two. Cross-multiplying for that comparison leaves twice the numerator against the denominator, which is the rule.

So the benchmark is not a separate fact but the comparison rule applied to the simplest possible fraction.

## Choosing the benchmark

Halves, quarters and whole numbers are the benchmarks worth knowing by sight, since estimates are usually made against them. A fraction near a half is near half the whole, and knowing which side is enough for an estimate and for a check.

Benchmarks are also what makes a fraction's size visible on a number line, since the positions of the common ones are easy to place and the others fall between them.
