# The z-score

## Standard deviations from the mean

A z-score says how many standard deviations a value lies from the mean, and in which direction. It is the difference from the mean divided by the standard deviation, so a value one standard deviation above the mean has a z-score of one.

The division is what makes values from different distributions comparable: a height and a test score have nothing in common until both are expressed in their own standard deviations.

## Standardising

Subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation turns any distribution into one with mean zero and standard deviation one, and that operation is called standardising. Every z-score is a value of the standardised version of its own data.

The standard normal table is exactly the table of z-scores, which is why the transformation is the first step of any normal-probability question.

## What the sign and size mean

A positive z-score is above the mean and a negative one below, and the magnitude says how unusual the value is. In a roughly normal distribution, about two thirds of the values have z-scores between minus one and one, and almost all are within three.

That is why a z-score of three is worth commenting on and a z-score of a half is not, regardless of the units the original measurement was in.
