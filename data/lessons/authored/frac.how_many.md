# How many fractions fit into a whole

## Division as a count

How many times a fraction fits into a whole number is a division: the whole number divided by the fraction. Reading the question as a count rather than as an operation is what makes the answer's size obvious before it is computed.

If the fraction is smaller than one, a whole number contains several of them, and the count is larger than the whole number. That is a useful check: dividing by something less than one increases the value.

## Multiplying by the reciprocal

Dividing by a fraction multiplies by its reciprocal, so the count is the whole number times the denominator divided by the numerator. The arithmetic is one multiplication and one division, and the order does not matter.

Writing the division as a multiplication is what turns a question that looks unfamiliar into one the learner already knows how to do.

## When the answer is whole

The count is a whole number exactly when the numerator divides the whole number times the denominator. When it does not, the answer is a fraction, and it means the last copy of the fraction is only partly used.

That partial copy is worth pointing out, because it is the difference between a count that comes out even and one that has a remainder.
