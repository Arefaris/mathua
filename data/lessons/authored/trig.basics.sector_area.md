# Sector area

## A slice of the circle

A sector is the region an arc sweeps out along with the two radii that reach its ends. It is a
slice of the circle, and its area is the same fraction of the whole circle that its central angle
is of a full turn.

That proportion is the whole idea. An angle of \(\pi/3\) is one sixth of \(2\pi\), so the sector is
one sixth of the circle's area.

## One half r squared theta

Sector area is half the radius squared times the angle in radians:
\(A = \tfrac{1}{2} r^{2} \theta\). The \(r^{2}\) matches the units of an area and the angle
supplies the fraction, with the same \(\tfrac{1}{2}\) that makes a triangle half its bounding
rectangle.

A radius of 6 and an angle of \(\pi/3\) give \(A = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 36 \cdot \pi/3 = 6\pi\).
As with arc length, the angle must be in radians for the formula to need no correction.

## The special case of a whole circle

Setting the angle to a full turn, \(2\pi\) radians, turns the sector back into the entire circle.
The formula gives \(A = \tfrac{1}{2} r^{2} (2\pi) = \pi r^{2}\), which is the familiar area.

That is a useful check rather than a coincidence. The sector formula is the circle area formula
restricted to a fraction, and the fraction becomes one for a full turn.
