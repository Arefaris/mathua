# Estimating square roots

Most square roots are not whole numbers. An estimate places the root between two consecutive whole numbers.

## Find the nearest squares

List the perfect squares around the radicand. For 50, the nearby squares are \(7^2 = 49\) and \(8^2 = 64\). The radicand 50 lies between them.

A perfect square has an exact whole-number root. If the radicand is itself a perfect square, the root is that whole number and there is no gap to bracket.

## Bracket the radicand

The order of squares carries through to the roots. Since \(49 < 50 < 64\),

\[
7 < \sqrt{50} < 8.
\]

The root is greater than 7 because 50 is greater than 49, and less than 8 because 50 is less than 64. The two bounds are consecutive whole numbers.

## Name the two integers

Report the smaller bound first, then the larger one: 7 and 8. This answers "between which two consecutive whole numbers" without pretending the root is exactly 7 or 8.

The estimate also gives a size check. \(\sqrt{50}\) is close to 7 because 50 is close to 49. It is not close to 8. For \(\sqrt{137}\), the bounds are 11 and 12, since \(121 < 137 < 144\).
