# The sum of the divisors

## Each prime contributes a series

The divisors of a prime power are the powers below it, so their sum is a geometric series. For the whole number, every divisor is a product of one choice from each prime's list, and the sum over all of them factors accordingly.

That factorisation is what turns a sum over many divisors into a product of small sums.

## Multiplying the series

Each prime contributes the series one plus p plus p squared up to its power, and the sum of the divisors is the product of those series. Expanding the product produces every combination once, which is exactly the divisor list.

The formula is finite and has a closed form, which is why a divisor sum can be computed from a factorisation rather than by enumeration.

## Perfect numbers

A number equals the sum of its proper divisors exactly when the divisor sum is twice the number. That is the definition of a perfect number, and it turns a question about a list of divisors into one equation.

The Euclid-Euler theorem then characterises the even perfect numbers by Mersenne primes, which is the historical reason this sum was studied at all.
