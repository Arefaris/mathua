# Volume elements in cylindrical and spherical coordinates

## Cylindrical

Cylindrical coordinates replace the plane's polar coordinates and keep the height:

\[dV = r\,dr\,d\theta\,dz.\]

The factor \(r\) is the same one the polar area element carries, because moving through a small angle traces an arc whose length grows with the radius. At \(r = 4\) the factor is 4.

## Spherical

Spherical coordinates use a radius and two angles, and the volume element is

\[dV = \rho^2 \sin\varphi\,d\rho\,d\varphi\,d\theta.\]

The factor contains a sine because a small change in the polar angle sweeps an arc of length proportional to \(\sin\varphi\): it is largest at the equator and vanishes at the poles. At \(\rho = 3\) and \(\varphi = \pi/2\) the factor is 9.

## Choosing coordinates

The coordinates are chosen so the region's boundary is constant in them. A cylinder is easy in cylindrical coordinates, a ball or a cone in spherical, and a box in rectangular, and the volume element carries whatever distortion the choice introduces.

Getting the element wrong is the usual mistake, since the limits can be right and the answer still wrong by the missing factor.
