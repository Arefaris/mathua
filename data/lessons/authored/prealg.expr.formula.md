# Evaluating a formula

A formula is an equation that states a relationship between quantities. Evaluating a formula means substituting given values for its variables and calculating the result.

## Substitute the given values

Replace each variable with its value, and use parentheses to keep the structure visible. For the perimeter formula \(P = 2l + 2w\), with \(l = 7\) and \(w = 4\),

\[
P = 2(7) + 2(4).
\]

The parentheses show that each variable is multiplied by 2 before the addition.

## Follow the order of operations

Multiply before adding:

\[
2(7) + 2(4) = 14 + 8 = 22.
\]

The perimeter is 22. For the triangle-area formula \(A = \frac{bh}{2}\), multiply the base and height first, then divide by 2. For the distance formula \(d = rt\), multiply the rate and the time.

## Keep the units and the meaning

The result has the unit of the quantity being found: perimeter in linear units, area in square units, and distance in the units of rate times time. A formula is a general relationship, and substituting values produces one instance of it. Check that the size of the result is reasonable for the units.
