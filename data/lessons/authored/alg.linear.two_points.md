# A line through two points

Two points determine a line, and the slope-intercept form needs two numbers: the slope and the intercept. Both come from the coordinates, and the subtraction order must match on top and bottom.

## Slope first

The slope is the change in \(y\) divided by the change in \(x\):

\[
m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}.
\]

Subtract the coordinates in the same order in the numerator and the denominator. Reversing one subtraction and not the other changes the sign, which is the commonest error in this topic.

## Point into the equation

Write \(y=mx+b\) and substitute one known point. With \(m\) already known, the only unknown is \(b\), so

\[
b=y_1-mx_1.
\]

Either point gives the same \(b\). A useful check is to substitute the second point as well; if the equation fails there, the slope or the intercept is wrong.

## Checking the line

A finished answer should pass both points and show the slope read from the equation. Substitute both coordinates: the two sides must be equal. If the question asks only for the right-hand side, enter the expression and not the whole equation.

The intercept is where the line crosses the \(y\)-axis, so it is the value of \(y\) at \(x=0\). That is exactly what \(b\) measures, and it is why the point \((0,b)\) is always on the line.
