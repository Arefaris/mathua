# Sum and difference of cubes

A cube plus a cube and a cube minus a cube each factor into a linear factor and a quadratic factor. The patterns are similar but not identical, and the sign inside the quadratic is the part that is easy to get wrong.

## Recognize the cubes

Write each term as a cube. The number 8 is \(2^3\), 27 is \(3^3\), and \(x^3\) is already a cube. A sum has the form

\[
x^3+n^3,
\]

and a difference has the form

\[
x^3-n^3.
\]

The exponent in the quadratic factor is always two, and its constant is \(n^2\). That constant is positive in both patterns.

## Apply the pattern

The factorizations are

\[
x^3+n^3=(x+n)(x^2-nx+n^2)
\]

and

\[
x^3-n^3=(x-n)(x^2+nx+n^2).
\]

The linear factor keeps the sign of the original expression. The middle term of the quadratic takes the opposite sign, and the last term is always positive. Multiplying the two factors back is the reliable check.

## Which factor the question wants

These questions ask for one factor, not the whole factorization. Read the wording: "the quadratic factor" means \(x^2\mp nx+n^2\), and "the linear factor" means \(x\pm n\). The two are different expressions, so the grader can tell them apart.

If the question asks for the quadratic factor, the answer starts with \(x^2\), never with \(x\).
