# Interpreting a remainder

A division with a remainder answers two different questions: how many full groups fit, and how many items are left over. The wording decides which number the problem wants.

## Divide and name the remainder

For 53 cookies packed 6 to a bag, divide:

\[
53 \div 6 = 8 \text{ remainder } 5.
\]

Eight bags can be filled, and 5 cookies remain. The remainder is always smaller than the divisor. If it is not, another full group fits.

## Drop the remainder for full groups

If the question asks how many full bags can be filled, only complete groups count. The 5 leftover cookies do not make a full bag, so the answer is 8.

This is not rounding the quotient down by habit. It is answering a question about complete groups, where a partial group does not qualify.

## Round up when every item must fit

If the question asks how many bags are needed to hold all 53 cookies, the 5 leftovers still need a bag. Eight bags hold 48 cookies. One more bag holds the remaining 5, so the answer is 9.

Check the choice against the wording. "Full bags" keeps the quotient. "Bags needed" or "containers required" adds one when the remainder is not zero.
