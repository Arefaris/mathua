# Order of operations with fractions

## Multiplication first

Fractions obey the same order of operations as whole numbers, so a multiplication inside a sum is done first. In a sum of a fraction and a product, the product is computed before the addition.

That order is the whole content of the rule, and it is the same rule the learner has already met with integers.

## Then a common denominator

After the product is computed, the remaining step is an addition of fractions, which needs a common denominator. The sum is then reduced to lowest terms.

Keeping the two steps separate is what makes the arithmetic manageable: combine the products first, then find one common denominator rather than several.

## What happens if you add first

Adding before multiplying gives a different, wrong answer, and the difference is easy to demonstrate with small numbers. That demonstration is worth doing once, because the rule reads as arbitrary until the two answers are compared.

The same discipline applies to brackets and to division, so the fraction case is a special case of a rule the learner already has.
