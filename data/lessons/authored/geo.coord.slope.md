# Slope from two points

## Rise over run

The slope of a line is its rise divided by its run: how much the height changes for each unit of horizontal movement. Between two points the rise is the difference in the y-coordinates and the run is the difference in the x-coordinates.

Subtract in the same order both times, or the sign comes out wrong: the first point's coordinate always goes second.

## From two points

For the points (1, 2) and (4, 8), the rise is 6 and the run is 3, so the slope is 2. The slope is the same whichever pair of points on the line is chosen, and that sameness is what makes the line straight.

A vertical line has a run of zero and no slope at all, which is why its equation is written as a constant \\(x\\) rather than in slope form.

## What the slope tells you

A positive slope rises to the right, a negative one falls, and a slope of zero is horizontal. The steeper the line, the larger the slope's magnitude, and two lines are parallel exactly when their slopes are equal.

The slope is also the rate of change when the axes carry quantities other than position: a slope of 60 on a distance-time graph means 60 units of distance per unit of time.
