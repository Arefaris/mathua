# Subtracting a fraction from a whole number

## Writing the whole as a fraction

A whole number is a fraction in disguise: writing it with the denominator of the fraction being subtracted turns the problem into a subtraction of two fractions. For a fraction with denominator four, the whole number one becomes four quarters.

Every whole number can be written over any denominator, and the version to choose is the one that matches the fraction already in the problem.

## Over a common denominator

With both numbers as fractions, the denominators agree and the numerators subtract in one line. The whole number's numerator is the denominator times the whole number, which is where the larger numerator comes from.

The result is usually not in lowest terms, so a reduction at the end is the last step.

## Checking the answer

The answer should be a little less than the whole number and positive, since a proper fraction was subtracted from it. An answer larger than the whole number means the whole was converted with the wrong numerator.

Converting to a common denominator and estimating the size are two checks that catch nearly every slip in this operation.
