# Similar figures and scale factors

## Same shape, different size

Two figures are similar when one is a scaled copy of the other: every angle matches and every length is multiplied by the same factor. The factor is the ratio of a side in one figure to its corresponding side in the other.

Similarity is what lets a map, a model and a photograph stand for the thing they depict, because shape is preserved even when size is not.

## The ratio is constant

For similar triangles the ratio of corresponding sides is the same for all three pairs. If the ratio of one pair is 2, and a side of the smaller triangle is 7, the corresponding side of the larger one is 14.

Matching the sides correctly is the whole difficulty: corresponding sides are the ones *between the same two angles*, not the ones that happen to look alike in a drawing.

## What similarity preserves

Similarity preserves angles and ratios of lengths, and it does not preserve lengths, areas or volumes. Areas scale by the square of the factor and volumes by its cube, which is why a model at one tenth scale has one hundredth of the surface area and one thousandth of the volume.
