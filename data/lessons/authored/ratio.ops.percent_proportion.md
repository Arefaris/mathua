# Percent problems with a proportion

A percent is a ratio out of 100. Writing a percent problem as a proportion keeps the part, the whole, and the percent in a consistent order and turns the question into an equation.

## Write the proportion

The basic proportion is

\[
\frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}} = \frac{\text{percent}}{100}.
\]

The part and the whole must be in the same order on both sides. If the part is 12 and the percent is \(15\%\), write \(\frac{12}{w} = \frac{15}{100}\), where \(w\) is the unknown whole.

## Cross-multiply

Multiply the numerator of one side by the denominator of the other:

\[
12 \times 100 = 15 \times w.
\]

So \(1200 = 15w\). Cross-multiplying removes the fractions and leaves a one-step equation.

## Solve for the missing value

Divide both sides by 15:

\[
w = \frac{1200}{15} = 80.
\]

The whole is 80. Check the result by finding \(15\%\) of 80: \(0.15 \times 80 = 12\), which is the original part. The same proportion works when the part or the percent is missing, as long as the unknown is placed in the correct position.
