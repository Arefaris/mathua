# Dilations

## Scaling about a point

A dilation has a centre and a factor. Every point moves along the ray from the centre through it, to a distance multiplied by the factor. A factor of 2 doubles every distance from the centre, and a factor of 1/3 divides them by three.

The centre itself is fixed, because a point at distance zero from the centre stays at distance zero.

## Factors below one

A factor between 0 and 1 shrinks the figure rather than enlarging it, and a negative factor also reflects it through the centre. Both cases are still dilations, and both preserve shape.

The image is similar to the original in every case, which is what makes a dilation the transformation that produces similar figures by construction rather than by comparison.

## Dilations and similar figures

Every pair of similar figures is related by a dilation together with a rigid motion, which is the modern definition of similarity: a dilation, possibly preceded by a rotation or a reflection. That is a stronger statement than "the same shape", because it says how the two are related.

It is also why lengths scale by the factor and areas by its square: the dilation applies the factor once per dimension.
