# The roots of the characteristic equation

## From the equation to the polynomial

Replacing each derivative in a constant-coefficient equation by a power of an unknown turns the equation into a polynomial. That polynomial is the characteristic equation, and its roots decide the solutions.

The substitution works because an exponential differentiates into a multiple of itself, so powers of the unknown stand in for derivatives.

## The coefficients as sums and products

For a quadratic characteristic equation the two roots sum to the negation of the linear coefficient over the leading one, and they multiply to the constant over the leading one. So the coefficients carry the answers without the roots being found.

That is why a question can ask about the sum or the product of the roots, and why the discriminant alone tells whether the solutions oscillate.

## Why the sum matters

The sum decides the sign of the real parts when the roots are complex, and therefore whether solutions grow or decay. The product decides whether the roots differ in sign, which decides the shape of the solution.

Reading the coefficients for those two facts is faster than solving, and it is what a stability question is really asking.
