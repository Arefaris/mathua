# Power of a product

A product raised to a power applies that power to every factor. The rule is often written \((ab)^n = a^n b^n\).

## Raise each factor

For \((3x)^2\), the base of the power is the product \(3x\). Squaring the product means multiplying it by itself:

\[
(3x)^2 = (3x)(3x) = 9x^2.
\]

The coefficient 3 is squared along with the variable. Writing \(3x^2\) would square only the variable and leave the coefficient unchanged, which is a different expression.

## Multiply the powers

The rule distributes the exponent over each factor:

\[
(ab)^n = a^n b^n.
\]

For \((4x)^3\), cube the coefficient and cube the variable separately. \(4^3 = 64\), and \(x^3\) stays \(x^3\), so the result is \(64x^3\).

The exponent does not add to itself. \((4x)^3\) is not \(4x^3\) and not \(12x^3\). The 4 is raised to the third power.

## Check with numbers

Replace the variable with 1. The expression \((4x)^3\) becomes \(4^3 = 64\), and the simplified form \(64x^3\) becomes 64. The two agree.

Try another value, such as \(x = 2\). The original is \(8^3 = 512\), and the simplified form is \(64 \times 8 = 512\). Agreement at two values is a quick check that the coefficient and exponent were handled together.
