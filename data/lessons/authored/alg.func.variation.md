# Direct and inverse variation

Variation describes a relationship of the form \(y=kx\) or \(y=k/x\), where \(k\) is a fixed constant. One data pair determines \(k\), and then the relationship answers any other question.

## Find the constant

For direct variation, \(y=kx\), so \(k=y/x\). For inverse variation, \(xy=k\), so \(k=xy\).

The constant has units and a meaning, but the arithmetic is just division or multiplication of the given pair. Keep the pair together: the \(x\) that goes with a \(y\) is the one that determines \(k\).

## Apply it to the new value

With \(k\) known, substitute the new \(x\). Direct variation multiplies: \(y=kx\). Inverse variation divides: \(y=k/x\).

The two formulas are not interchangeable. Using the direct formula on inverse data gives an answer that grows when it should shrink.

## Two different relationships

In direct variation, doubling \(x\) doubles \(y\), and the graph is a line through the origin. In inverse variation, doubling \(x\) halves \(y\), and the graph is a hyperbola.

The constant is the product \(xy\) in the inverse case and the ratio \(y/x\) in the direct case. Checking that the given pair and the answer pair share the same constant is the fastest verification.
