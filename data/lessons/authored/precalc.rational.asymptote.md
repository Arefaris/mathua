# Asymptotes of a rational function

## Vertical asymptotes

A rational function has a vertical asymptote where its denominator vanishes and its numerator does not. Solving the denominator for zero gives the location, and the graph rises or falls without bound on each side of it.

For \(f(x) = (3x + 1)/(2x - 8)\) the denominator vanishes at \(x = 4\), so that is the vertical asymptote. If the numerator vanished there too, the common factor would cancel and the graph would have a hole instead of an asymptote.

## Horizontal asymptotes

The end behaviour is decided by the degrees. A denominator of higher degree gives the horizontal asymptote \(y = 0\); equal degrees give the ratio of the leading coefficients; a numerator of higher degree gives no horizontal asymptote, though there may be an oblique one.

For \((2x^2 + 5)/(4x^2 - 1)\) the degrees are equal, so the horizontal asymptote is \(y = 2/4 = 1/2\).

## Sketching

The two kinds of asymptote, the intercepts, and the sign of the function between the vertical asymptotes are enough to sketch the shape without plotting many points. The signs are what say which way each branch leaves.

A graph that crosses its horizontal asymptote is possible and common, because the asymptote describes the end behaviour only, not a barrier the curve cannot cross.
