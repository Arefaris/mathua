# Power of a quotient

A fraction raised to a power applies that power to the numerator and to the denominator. The rule is \((x/y)^n = x^n / y^n\), provided \(y\) is not zero.

## Raise numerator and denominator

For \((x/3)^2\), the base is the quotient \(x/3\). Squaring it means

\[
\left(\frac{x}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{x^2}{3^2} = \frac{x^2}{9}.
\]

The numerator and denominator are treated separately. The exponent does not apply to the fraction as a single number in some other way.

## Keep the fraction

The denominator is a number, so evaluate its power. \(3^2 = 9\), and the result stays a fraction: \(x^2/9\). Do not move the 9 into the numerator.

For \((x/4)^3\), cube the numerator and the denominator: \(x^3/64\). The denominator grows quickly because it is raised to the same power as the numerator.

## Check the denominator

The denominator must not be zero. In \((x/3)^2\), the original fraction already excludes values that make the denominator zero. Here the denominator is the constant 3, so the expression is defined for every \(x\).

Check with \(x = 6\). The original is \((6/3)^2 = 2^2 = 4\), and the simplified form is \(36/9 = 4\). Agreement confirms that both parts were raised to the power.
