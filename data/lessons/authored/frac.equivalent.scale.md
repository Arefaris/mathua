# Equivalent fractions by scaling

## Multiplying top and bottom

Multiplying a fraction's numerator and denominator by the same number gives an equivalent fraction: the value is unchanged because the fraction is multiplied by one in the form of that number over itself.

Two thirds may be written as eight twelfths, and both name the same point on the number line.

## Choosing the factor

To reach a chosen denominator, the factor is that denominator divided by the original one, and the numerator is multiplied by the same factor. The division has to come out exactly, which is why the chosen denominator must be a multiple of the original.

Choosing a denominator that is not a multiple is the usual error, and it produces a fraction with a fractional numerator rather than an equivalent fraction.

## Why the value stays the same

Multiplying by one never changes a value, and a number over itself is one whenever it is not zero. So the scaling is a multiplication by one and the fraction is the same number, which is the entire justification.

The idea runs in both directions: the same operation makes a fraction easier to compare with another, and the reverse operation reduces it to lowest terms.
