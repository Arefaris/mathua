# Cyclic quadrilaterals

## Opposite angles are supplementary

A quadrilateral whose four vertices all lie on a circle is cyclic, and its opposite angles add to 180 degrees. That is a strong constraint: once one angle is known, so is the one across the circle.

The condition also runs backwards: a quadrilateral with opposite angles summing to 180 has a circle through its four vertices, which is the usual way to prove four points are concyclic.

## Why it holds

Each angle of the quadrilateral is inscribed in the circle, so each is half the arc it subtends. Opposite angles subtend the two arcs that make up the whole circle, and the two halves together are half of 360, which is 180.

That is the inscribed-angle theorem applied twice, which is why the two concepts sit next to each other.

## Special cases

A rectangle is cyclic, and its opposite angles are both right angles, so they sum to 180 trivially. An isosceles trapezoid is cyclic as well, which is another way to characterise it.

A parallelogram that is not a rectangle is not cyclic, because its opposite angles are equal rather than supplementary; only when they are both right angles do the two conditions agree.
