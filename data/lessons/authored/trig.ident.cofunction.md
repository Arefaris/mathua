# Cofunction identities

## Complements in a right triangle

A right triangle has one angle of \(90^{\circ}\), so its other two angles must add to \(90^{\circ}\).
Each is the complement of the other, and that pairing is the source of the cofunction identities.

The two acute angles see the same three sides from opposite ends. What is opposite one angle is
adjacent to the other, and the hypotenuse is shared.

## Sine and cosine swap

That exchange of opposite and adjacent is exactly the swap of sine and cosine. For an acute
\(\theta\),

\(\sin\theta = \cos(90^{\circ} - \theta)\) and \(\cos\theta = \sin(90^{\circ} - \theta)\).

The same pattern runs through the other pairs: tangent pairs with cotangent and secant with
cosecant, each with the complementary angle. Given \(\sin(32^{\circ}) = \cos(\varphi)\), the
identity forces \(\varphi = 90^{\circ} - 32^{\circ} = 58^{\circ}\).

## Why the name co-sine

The prefix "co" is short for complement. Cosine was named as the sine of the complementary angle,
which is why the identity is not a coincidence but a restatement of the definition.

Seeing the name that way also explains what the identities are for. They turn a function of an angle
larger than \(45^{\circ}\) into a function of a smaller one, which is how a trigonometric table was
shortened by half.
