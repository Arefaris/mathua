# Arc length

## Radians measure the arc

A radian is defined by the circle itself: one radian is the angle that cuts an arc as long as the
radius. That definition is what makes radians the natural unit for arc length, because the angle in
radians and the arc it cuts on a unit circle are literally the same number.

So an angle of 2 radians on a circle of radius 1 cuts an arc of length 2. Scaling the
circle scales the arc, and nothing else changes.

## The arc length formula

Arc length is the radius times the angle in radians: \(s = r\theta\). The radius sets the size of
the circle and the angle sets the fraction of it that the arc covers, so their product is the
length. A radius of 6 with an angle of \(\pi/3\) gives \(s = 6 \cdot \pi/3 = 2\pi\).

The angle has to be in radians for this to work without a correction term. Mixing a degree measure
into the formula gives an arc far too long, because a degree is a smaller unit than the formula
expects.

## Why degrees would need a factor

The radian formula is the clean one, and the degree version shows why. In degrees the same relation
is \(s = \pi r \theta / 180\), where the fraction \(\pi/180\) is exactly the conversion from degrees
to radians.

So the formula does not change; the unit does. Working in radians keeps the conversion factor at 1 and leaves \(s = r\theta\) free to be read off directly.
