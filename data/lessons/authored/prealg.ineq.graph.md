# Graphing an inequality

The graph of an inequality shows every number that makes the inequality true. On a number line, the graph has a boundary point and a shaded side.

## Draw the boundary

Locate the boundary number on the number line. Use an open circle for \(<\) or \(>\), because the boundary is not part of the solution. Use a closed circle for \(\le\) or \(\ge\), because the boundary is included.

For \(x \ge -2\), draw a closed circle at \(-2\).

## Shade the solution side

For \(x > a\) or \(x \ge a\), shade to the right, where the numbers are larger. For \(x < a\) or \(x \le a\), shade to the left, where the numbers are smaller. The circle and the direction are two separate decisions.

The graph of \(x \ge -2\) is a closed circle at \(-2\) with shading to the right.

## Check a test point

Pick a number in the shaded region and substitute it into the inequality. For \(x \ge -2\), test 0: \(0 \ge -2\) is true. Pick a number on the unshaded side, such as \(-3\): \(-3 \ge -2\) is false. The test confirms both the circle and the direction.
