# A definite integral by substitution

## Changing the limits

A definite integral by substitution changes the limits along with the variable: when the variable is replaced, the limits are expressed in the new variable too. So a limit of zero in x becomes the corresponding value of the substituted expression, and the two need not match.

Skipping that step and substituting back at the end is the longer route, and it is where most errors enter.

## Why the factor has to be there

Substitution is the chain rule read backwards, so the derivative of the inner function has to appear in the integrand. When it does, the substitution turns the integral into one of the standard form; when it does not, another method is needed.

That is why an integrand is inspected for a factor that is the derivative of something inside it rather than substituted blindly.

## Checking by differentiating

An antiderivative can always be checked by differentiating it, and that check costs one line. For a definite integral the check is on the antiderivative rather than the number, so it catches the substitution error rather than an arithmetic one.

The two checks are worth separating: a right antiderivative with a wrong evaluation is a different mistake from a wrong antiderivative.
