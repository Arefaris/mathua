# Powers with negative bases

A negative sign in a power can belong to the base or stand outside the power. Parentheses decide which.

## Parentheses include the sign

In \((-2)^3\), the parentheses make \(-2\) the base. The exponent applies to the whole negative number:

\[
(-2)^3 = (-2)(-2)(-2) = -8.
\]

An even power of a negative base is positive, because the negative factors pair up. An odd power stays negative.

## No parentheses: the power acts first

In \(-2^3\), there are no parentheses around the negative sign. The exponent applies to 2 first, and the negative sign stays outside:

\[
-2^3 = -(2^3) = -8.
\]

This expression is negative for every exponent, even or odd, because the minus sign is applied after the power. The notation is a convention, not an optional detail. \(-2^2 = -4\), while \((-2)^2 = 4\).

## Even and odd powers

For a parenthesized negative base, count the factors. Two negative factors give a positive product, three give a negative product, and so on. For an unparenthesized expression, the sign is negative regardless of the exponent.

Read the parentheses before calculating. Most errors in this topic come from treating \(-2^2\) as if it meant \((-2)^2\).
