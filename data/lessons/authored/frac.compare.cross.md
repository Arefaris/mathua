# Comparing fractions by cross-multiplication

## Cross-multiplication

Two fractions can be compared by multiplying each numerator by the other denominator and comparing the two products. The larger product belongs to the larger fraction.

Doing it in that order puts both fractions over a common denominator without writing it, since the two products are the two numerators the common denominator would give.

## The sign tells you which is larger

The difference of the two products has the same sign as the difference of the fractions, so a positive difference means the first fraction is larger and a negative one means the second is. A zero difference means they are equal.

Using the difference rather than the two products separately is what makes the comparison a single computation.

## Why it works

Multiplying both sides of a comparison by the product of the two positive denominators does not change the direction of the inequality, and it clears both fractions at once. That is the whole argument, and it is why the method needs both denominators to be positive.

For negative fractions the same arithmetic works but the direction of the inequality is worth checking explicitly rather than assumed.
