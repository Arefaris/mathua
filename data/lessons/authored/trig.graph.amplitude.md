# Amplitude

## How far a wave swings

A sine or cosine graph oscillates about a middle line, and the amplitude is how far it rises above
that line or falls below it. The wave reaches the midline plus the amplitude at each peak and the
midline minus the amplitude at each trough.

The amplitude is therefore one number that describes the size of the swing, independent of where the
wave starts or how quickly it repeats.

## Amplitude is a distance

In \(y = a\sin(x)\) the amplitude is \(|a|\), the absolute value of the coefficient in front of the
function. The absolute value is not a technicality: amplitude measures a distance, and a distance is
never negative.

A coefficient of -4 gives an amplitude of 4. The minus sign reflects the wave over the
horizontal axis, swapping peaks and troughs, but a peak of height 4 below the midline is still
4 from it.

## What amplitude is not

The coefficient does not set the period. A multiplier inside the function, as in \(\sin(bx)\),
changes how many cycles fit into a fixed interval, and that is a separate quantity from how tall
each cycle is.

Keeping the two separate is the point of naming them. Amplitude is vertical size; period is
horizontal spacing; they are set by different parts of the expression and can be changed one at a
time.
