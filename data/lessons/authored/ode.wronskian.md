# The Wronskian

## A determinant that tests independence

The Wronskian of two functions is the determinant of the matrix of the functions and their derivatives. It is non-zero at a point exactly when the two are independent there, so a single number answers a question about independence.

That is its purpose: two solutions of a second-order equation are a basis precisely when their Wronskian does not vanish.

## The two powers case

For two powers the determinant collapses to a single term: the difference of the exponents times a power. Most of the algebra cancels because differentiating a power only changes its coefficient.

The power form is worth doing by hand once, since the general Wronskian is rarely computed in a first course.

## A non-zero value

A non-zero value at any point is enough to conclude independence on the interval, because solutions of a linear equation either have a Wronskian that vanishes everywhere or one that vanishes nowhere.

That dichotomy is the reason a single evaluation settles the question, and it is a property of linear equations rather than of determinants.
