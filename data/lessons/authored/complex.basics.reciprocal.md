# The reciprocal of a complex number

The reciprocal of \(z = a + bi\) is another complex number, found by multiplying above and below by the conjugate \(a - bi\). The denominator becomes \(a^2 + b^2\), which is a positive real number, and the result is real and imaginary parts over that denominator.

## Multiplying by the conjugate

The move is \(\frac{1}{a+bi} = \frac{a-bi}{(a+bi)(a-bi)}\), and the denominator simplifies because \((a+bi)(a-bi) = a^2 + b^2\). That number is the squared modulus, \(|z|^2\), so the reciprocal is the conjugate divided by the squared modulus.

For \(3 + 4i\): the conjugate is \(3 - 4i\) and the denominator is \(9 + 16 = 25\), so \(\frac{1}{3+4i} = \frac{3}{25} - \frac{4}{25}i\).

## The formula

In one line, \(\frac{1}{a+bi} = \frac{a}{a^2+b^2} - \frac{b}{a^2+b^2}i\). The parts are the conjugate's parts divided by the squared modulus, and the sign of the imaginary part flips because the conjugate flips it.

The formula never divides by zero: \(a^2 + b^2 = 0\) only when \(a = b = 0\), and that is the one complex number with no reciprocal.

## What the answer looks like

The answer is a complex number, not a fraction standing for one, and it can be written with rational coefficients. The reciprocal of \(2 + i\) is \(\frac{2}{5} - \frac{1}{5}i\), and the reciprocal of \(1 + i\) is \(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2}i\).

Decimals describe the same numbers, so \(0.12 - 0.16i\) is the reciprocal of \(3 + 4i\) as well. The check is multiplication: \((3+4i)(0.12 - 0.16i)\) should be 1.
