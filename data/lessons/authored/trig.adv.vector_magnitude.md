# The magnitude of a vector

## A vector as an arrow

A vector such as \(\langle 6, 8 \rangle\) is a displacement: 6 along one axis and 8 along the other,
with the order fixed. Written that way it has no single number attached, but it does have a length,
which is what the magnitude reports.

The magnitude is the distance from the tail to the tip, and it is a number rather than a pair.

## The length from the components

The two components are the legs of a right triangle whose hypotenuse runs from the tail to the tip,
so the magnitude is the Pythagorean hypotenuse: \(|\langle x, y \rangle| = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}\).
For \(\langle 6, 8 \rangle\) that is \(\sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10\).

The formula is the distance formula with one endpoint at the origin, for the same reason: a
displacement and a distance are both differences of coordinates.

## Signed components do not change it

Squaring before adding is what makes the signs irrelevant. \(\langle -6, 8 \rangle\) and
\(\langle 6, 8 \rangle\) point into different quadrants and have the same magnitude, and
\(\langle 3, 4 \rangle\) and \(\langle -3, -4 \rangle\) point in exactly opposite directions at the
same length.

That is also why a vector whose components come from a right triangle has a magnitude that is exact:
the hypotenuse of a triple is a whole number, so 10 is an answer and not an approximation.
