# The standard error

## The precision of an estimate

The standard error is the standard deviation of the sample mean, and it measures how precisely the mean has been estimated rather than how spread out the data is. A second sample would give a different mean, and the standard error is the typical size of that difference.

It is the number that appears in every confidence interval and test statistic about a mean, which is why it is worth separating from the standard deviation it is computed from.

## The square root of the sample size

The standard error is the sample's standard deviation divided by the square root of the sample size. The division is by the square root rather than the count, so quadrupling the sample halves the error: precision improves with more data, but slowly.

That slow improvement is why a study that wants to double its precision needs four times the data, and why very large samples are expensive for small gains.

## Comparing with the standard deviation

The standard deviation describes one observation and does not change as the sample grows, since it is a property of the data. The standard error describes the estimate of the mean and shrinks as the sample grows.

Confusing the two is the most common error in reading a reported result, and the units are the same, so the mistake is invisible without the distinction.
