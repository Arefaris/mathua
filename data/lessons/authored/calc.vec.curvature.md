# Curvature

## The formula

Curvature measures how sharply a curve turns and does not depend on how fast it is traced. For a graph \(y = f(x)\),

\[\kappa = \frac{|y''|}{\left(1 + (y')^2\right)^{3/2}}.\]

The second derivative supplies the turning and the denominator converts a change in slope into a change in direction: a steep slope needs a larger second derivative to turn through the same angle.

## A parabola at its vertex

For \(y = ax^2\) the vertex is at \(x = 0\), where \(y' = 0\) and \(y'' = 2a\). The denominator is 1 and the curvature is \(2a\): the larger the coefficient, the more sharply the parabola bends at its vertex.

Away from the vertex the slope term grows, so the same parabola is less curved there even though its second derivative is unchanged.

## Circles

A circle of radius \(r\) has curvature \(1/r\) at every point, which is the case that names the quantity: curvature is the reciprocal of the radius of the circle that best fits the curve there. A straight line has zero curvature, and a large circle has almost none.

That reciprocal relationship is what makes curvature the right measure for a curve that is traced at varying speed, and it is why the parametrised form of the formula divides by the cube of the speed.
