# Markup and selling price

A markup is an amount a store adds to its cost before selling an item. The selling price is the cost plus the markup. Markup is calculated from the cost, so the cost is the whole.

## Markup is added to cost

The selling price has two parts:

\[
\text{selling price} = \text{cost} + \text{markup}.
\]

A store that buys an item for \(\$80\) and marks it up by \(25\%\) adds a markup to that \(\$80\).

## Find the markup

The markup is the markup rate multiplied by the cost:

\[
\text{markup} = 0.25 \times 80 = 20.
\]

The markup is \(\$20\). The rate is applied to the cost, not to the selling price. Applying it to the selling price would be a margin calculation, which is a different question.

## Add to get the selling price

Add the markup to the cost:

\[
80 + 20 = 100.
\]

The selling price is \(\$100\). A positive markup rate always makes the selling price greater than the cost. Check the answer by subtracting the cost from the selling price: \(100 - 80 = 20\), which is \(25\%\) of the cost.
