# Unit fractions

## Fractions with numerator one

A unit fraction has 1 as its numerator: a half, a third, a quarter. Every fraction is a sum of unit fractions, and some are sums of a few of them.

Writing a fraction as such a sum is the oldest way of recording fractions, and it makes the size of each part visible.

## Decomposing one fraction

Given a fraction written as a half plus a unit fraction, the missing denominator is found by putting the sum over a common denominator and matching the two forms. The arithmetic is two lines rather than a search.

More than two terms can be needed in general, and the greedy method, subtract the largest unit fraction that fits and repeat, always terminates, though it does not always give the shortest sum.

## Why it is worth doing

Unit fractions are how quantities are shared in words: a third of the group, a quarter of the tank. Expressing a complicated fraction as a sum of those is what makes it comparable to the quantities being discussed.

The idea returns in infinite series, where a sum of unit fractions is the standard way to build one that converges, and in number theory, where the question of how few unit fractions suffice is still studied.
