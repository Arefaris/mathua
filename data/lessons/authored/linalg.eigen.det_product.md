# The determinant as a product of eigenvalues

## The determinant

The determinant measures the factor by which a matrix scales signed area, and it is computed from the entries by the cofactor expansion or the row-reduction rules.

It equals the product of the eigenvalues, counting multiplicity, which is the second of the two invariants a matrix carries.

## Why it is the product

The characteristic polynomial has the determinant as its constant term, with a sign, and the constant term of a polynomial is the product of its roots up to the leading coefficient. Setting the two readings against each other gives the identity.

For a triangular matrix the entries on the diagonal are the eigenvalues, so the product is immediate: the determinant is the product of the diagonal entries.

## Both invariants together

The trace gives the sum of the eigenvalues and the determinant their product, so for a two-by-two matrix the pair determines them completely. Knowing one eigenvalue and either invariant gives the other, which is why questions about a single unknown eigenvalue are usually one subtraction or one division.

A determinant of zero says one eigenvalue is zero, which means the matrix collapses a direction, which is the same fact as it having no inverse.
