# The area of a trapezoid

## The average of the parallel sides

A trapezoid has one pair of parallel sides, and its area is the average of those two sides times the height:

\[
A = \frac{b_1 + b_2}{2}\,h.
\]

For parallel sides of 6 and 10 and a height of 4, the average is 8 and the area is 32.

## Why the average

Two copies of the trapezoid, one rotated, fit together into a parallelogram whose base is the sum of the two parallel sides and whose height is the same. That parallelogram's area is \((b_1+b_2)h\), and one trapezoid is half of it, which is the formula.

The argument is worth keeping because it also gives the triangle formula: a triangle is a trapezoid with one parallel side of length zero, and the average of zero and the base is half the base.

## Special cases

When the two parallel sides are equal the figure is a parallelogram and the formula is base times height. When one is zero it is a triangle. The trapezoid formula therefore contains the other two as special cases, which is a reason to learn it first rather than as a separate rule.

The height must be perpendicular to the parallel sides, not the length of a slanted leg; using the leg is the standard error.
