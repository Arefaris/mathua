# The mass of a lamina

## Density times area

A flat plate with a uniform density has a mass equal to the density multiplied by its area. The units multiply the same way: mass per unit area times area leaves mass.

That is the whole computation when the density is constant, and it is the case worth recognising so that the calculus is reserved for the cases that need it.

## When the density varies

A real plate is rarely uniform, and the mass is then the double integral of the density over the region. The integral is the limit of a sum of small pieces, each with its own local density, which is why it generalises the multiplication rather than replacing it.

Reading a double integral of a density as a mass is what makes the multivariable integral physical rather than formal.

## Reading the units

The integral of a density over an area has units of mass, and the integral of a density over a length has units of mass as well, over a different shape. The units are the check that the right kind of integral was set up: an area integral of a linear density would not be a mass.

Checking the units before integrating is fast, and it catches a wrong measure more often than the algebra does.
