# The triangle inequality

## The inequality

Any two sides of a triangle together are longer than the third:

\[
a + b > c
\]

for every assignment of the three sides. A set of three lengths that fails this for even one pairing cannot be assembled into a triangle at all.

## The smallest and largest sides

Given two sides, the inequality bounds the third from both directions. It must be shorter than their sum and longer than their difference. For sides of 5 and 8, the third side lies strictly between 3 and 13.

Working in whole numbers, the largest possible third side is 12 and the smallest is 4, and both bounds come from the single inequality read in the two directions.

## Why it holds

The shortest path between two points is the straight line, so going from one endpoint to the other by way of the third vertex can only be longer. That is the whole proof, and it is also why the inequality is strict: the equality case is a degenerate triangle whose vertices lie on one line.

The same reasoning gives the distance formula in the plane, and it is why a route through a waypoint is never shorter than the direct route.
