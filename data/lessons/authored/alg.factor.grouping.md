# Factoring by grouping

A four-term polynomial can sometimes be split into two pairs that share a binomial. The shared binomial is then one factor, and the leftover parts combine into the other.

## Split into two pairs

Group the first two terms and the last two terms. The split is not arbitrary: it is chosen so that each pair has a common monomial factor.

For a polynomial such as

\[
ax^3+akx^2+cx+ck,
\]

the first pair shares \(ax^2\) and the second shares \(c\). The pairing is visible once each pair is written with its factor pulled out.

## Factor each pair

Factor the greatest common factor from each pair separately:

\[
ax^2(x+k)+c(x+k).
\]

The two remaining parentheses must be identical. If they are not, either the pairing is wrong or the polynomial does not factor by grouping with integer coefficients. Swapping the middle terms is the usual repair.

## Take out the shared binomial

The shared binomial \((x+k)\) is a common factor of the two products, so

\[
ax^2(x+k)+c(x+k)=(x+k)(ax^2+c).
\]

A question that asks for the shared binomial wants \(x+k\), not the whole factorization. Read the wording: entering the other factor is a different expression and is not equivalent to it.
