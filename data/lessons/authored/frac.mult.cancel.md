# Cancelling before multiplying

## The same factor in two places

A fraction multiplication can be simplified before it is carried out by cancelling a factor that appears in a numerator and in a denominator. The product is unchanged, because the cancelled factor contributes equally above and below the line.

Doing it first keeps the intermediate numbers small, which is what makes larger problems manageable by hand.

## Why it is allowed

Cancelling is dividing the numerator and denominator of the whole product by the same number, which never changes a fraction's value. Doing it term by term before multiplying is the same division applied earlier, so the result must agree.

The rule is therefore not a trick but the same equivalence that lets a single fraction be reduced, applied while the factors are still visible.

## Choosing between the routes

Multiplying first and reducing afterwards always works and is easier to describe. Cancelling first is faster and less prone to large-number slips, and the choice between them is a matter of the numbers involved rather than of correctness.

Either way the answer is in lowest terms at the end, and a check with an estimate catches the slip that cancelling wrongly would produce.
