# The interquartile range

## The middle half

The interquartile range is the distance between the upper and lower quartiles, so it measures the spread of the middle half of the data. Half the values lie within it: the quarter below the median and the quarter above.

Because it ignores the top and bottom quarters entirely, one extreme value does not change it.

## Splitting at the median

The lower quartile is the median of the values below the median, and the upper quartile is the median of the values above it. So the interquartile range is two medians and a subtraction, and no summing is involved.

The method is worth stating explicitly because the exact half to take depends on whether the count is even or odd, and different conventions exist for the odd case.

## Why it resists outliers

A single very large value moves the maximum and the mean but leaves the quartiles where they were, since it lands in the top quarter and does not change which values are in the middle. That is why it is reported beside the median rather than beside the mean.

For a distribution with a long tail, the median and interquartile range describe the typical value and its spread better than the mean and standard deviation do.
