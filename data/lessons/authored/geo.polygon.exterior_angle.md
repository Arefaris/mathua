# Exterior angles of a regular polygon

## Exterior angles add to 360

An exterior angle is formed by one side and the extension of the next. Walking around any convex polygon, the direction turns through the exterior angle at every vertex, and one full circuit is a turn of 360 degrees, so the exterior angles always add to 360.

That is a stronger and simpler fact than the interior-angle sum, and it needs no triangulation.

## One angle of a regular polygon

In a regular polygon the exterior angles are equal, so each is 360 divided by the number of sides. A regular octagon has exterior angles of \(360/8 = 45\) degrees.

The answer is a whole number of degrees only when the side count divides 360, which is a property of the number rather than of the geometry.

## Why the walk turns once

Following the boundary of a convex polygon and facing along each side, the heading changes by the exterior angle at each vertex. After visiting every vertex the direction has returned to where it started, having turned through a full circle.

For a polygon traversed clockwise the turns are negative, and the sum is \(-360\), so the statement is about the total turn rather than about the magnitudes when orientation is taken into account.
