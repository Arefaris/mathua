# ADR-044: `/learn` names its own units and withholds its own syllabus

*2026-10-06*

five chrome changes, each a case of the app describing itself in the codebase's vocabulary rather than the learner's. **`level 0.40`** was the generator's difficulty float in the header — not a goal, a score, or anything actionable, and it moved with every question, so it read as a fluctuating verdict on the learner; the server-decided band beside it stays, because that one means something. **`KP 2/5`** becomes "Section 2 of 5": *KP* is what `internal/concepts` calls the unit, and the learner's unit is a section. **"Work it through on paper first"** instructed a method before the learner had met the problem and asked them to do something the app cannot see them do. **Steps appear only after the section is attempted** — listed before the question they are a syllabus nobody has chosen, and the effect is to tick them off and skip the work they support. Derived from the append-only feed, so there is no state to keep in sync; keyed on a question having **feedback**, not on existing, because a `q` entry exists from the moment the question is *served*, which would have revealed the steps at the very instant the learner was about to try it unaided. **Prerequisites were already two-tier and correctly routed** — unseen → `/learn`, already demonstrated → `/review`, which is problems-first and fetches no lesson, the ADR-037 purgatory arriving through a different door — but the unseen half was labeled "**Review**", offering a tutorial under the name of a check. It now says Learn. Also fixed en route, and it is the reason the first version of that condition broke the whole suite: the `answeredSections` memo was written *below* the `if (loading && entries.length === 0)` early return, which React reports as "rendered more hooks than during the previous render" — and only once the guard stopped short, i.e. on the first successful load rather than in any test that renders a finished feed. `test/learnChrome.test.tsx` covers all five.
