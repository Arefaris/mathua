# ADR-034: MathLive is universal, and the keypad answers for the corpus rather than for arithmetic

*2026-10-03*

the editor had been scoped to the five expression-valued `grading_type`s, mirroring `internal/grader/router.go`, on the reasoning that MathLive earns its place only where the grader *parses* an expression. That left **223 of 657** concepts typing mathematics into a plain `<input>`, 209 `multiple_choice`, 11 `tuple`, 1 `ordering`, 2 `comparison`, with no fraction key, no exponent, no parentheses and no symbols, even though the answer hosts render a text field alongside the tap targets. **Measured before changing anything**, because the multiple-choice grader is `strings.EqualFold(expected, answer)` and a CAS rewrites what you type: MathLive reads bare adjacent letters as implicit multiplication, so `yes` came back `y e s`, `no` as `n o`, `odd` as `o d d`, `addition` as `a d d i t i o n` and `pi/2` as `p i/2`. With `yes` the most common expected answer in the corpus (673) and `no` second (171), universal MathLive would have broken ~850 questions. `scripts/record-mathlive-corpus.mjs` now records 10 multiple-choice shapes that did not exist before (the compat test's `pureGoTypes` listed `GradingMultipleChoice` but had **zero** cases, so that branch was never exercised), and two fixes close the gap: `fixImplicitLetterSpacing` rejoins a word split into letters, guarded so `2pi r` does not become `2pir`, by extending left over the whole letter sequence and refusing when a digit or operator precedes it, and `fixFractionParens` accepts a symbolic numerator (`(pi)/(2)`) while staying narrow enough to leave `(x + 1)/(y)` alone. Multiple choice still renders `ChoiceOptions` above the field, so yes/no stays a tap; the keypad is the typed path. The keypad had to grow to match: **no comma existed in any layout**, so every `tuple` and `ordering` answer was unenterable; and there were no `< > ≤ ≥` keycaps for the two comparison concepts. Three findings cost most of the time. **MathLive's toolbar navigates *layouts*, not *layers***, it emits `data-layer="${layout.layers[0].id}"` per entry in `kb.layouts`, so a three-`layers` layout renders only its first layer and layers 2 and 3 are unreachable; a keycap carrying a `layer` property merely gains a `layer-switch` *class* and does nothing when pressed. Pages are therefore separate single-layer layouts, `layoutsForMode` returns them together, and `kb.layouts = [...pages, 'alphabetic']`. Letters come from MathLive's own `alphabetic` layout rather than a 26-key grid Mathua would have to design for a 320px screen and fail the 44px floor on. And every layout needed a `label`, because the toolbar renders a layer's label and shows "untitled" without one. Layers 2+ had been unreachable before this too, the old operators/scientific-notation layer had no way in, the same class of bug as the keycap that typed its own name (ADR-029).
