# ADR-045: `/learn` teaches a slice, and never the article it was cut from

*2026-10-06*

**the empty `kp.section` had been falling back to the whole lesson body.** 69 of the 1,971 KPs the corpus then held across 43 concepts had no section; the fallback served them the full article, which for `discrete.logic.propositions` was a **2,724-word** page served three times, 8,172 words of reading before the first question. `/learn` is the loop (ADR-017/018/023: action → practice → explanation); `/study` is the reference (ADR-021). A reference page silently substituted into the loop makes the loop a reading exercise and defeats the only place the learner is held to answer. `Loader.TeachingSlice` replaces the fallback, and **`SectionSlice` returns nothing when there is no section** rather than something plausible, ADR-041's lesson, inverted: an empty card the learner can see is honest, a whole article is not. `Loader.SliceAudit` reports the backlog, `TestRealDataEveryEmptySectionConceptHasASliceOrMinimalBody` fails if an empty-section concept on a first card regresses to the article, and `TestRealDataSectionedSlicesStayInsideTheirSection` fails if a slice leaks a neighbouring heading's content. **Measured, and it set the authoring scope:** 33,784 words across those 69 KPs, and only **9 of 69** empty-section KPs had a lexical heading match, **60 needed authoring**, so a matcher would have left most of the corpus broken while reporting success. 33 slices are authored across the 11 concepts that appear as a first card, including the propositions one; 36 KPs across 32 concepts remain on the honest minimal placeholder, which names the concept and points at `/study` rather than pretending to teach. Existing sectioned content is reported, not truncated, **712 of 1,902 sections exceed 200 words** (median 145, max 8,197), because automatic truncation would silently delete instruction, and a content backlog wearing a gate's clothes is ADR-041's second finding.
