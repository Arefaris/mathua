# ADR-038: Mastery is decided by evidence, and the ladder is three different questions

*2026-10-04*

completes ADR-037's deferred work. Mastery required `streak >= required_streak && avg time <= threshold` at **all three** tiers, and the engine resets the streak on every advance, so **459 of the 657 the corpus then held needed 30 consecutive correct answers** — 15,774 answers and ~130 hours for the corpus, median 12 min per concept, and a **4.2%** chance of completing a run in one sitting at 90% per-answer accuracy. One miss zeroes the streak. The evidence model this brief describes **already existed**: `web/next-app/lib/progression.ts:masteryEstimate` (recent-weighted accuracy × difficulty × variety of instance × time) whose comment reads "Streak is one input, not the decision" — but it rendered a label in the /learn header while the streak counter gated everything. `internal/mastery/evidence.go` is that model server-side, so there is one model instead of two that can disagree. `Machine.Next` now reads a `Rung` derived from it, and the three rungs are three different questions — which is what `docs/diagrams/student-model-dark.svg` always drew ("first correct", "streak reached", "interval elapsed") and what the implementation had collapsed into one condition repeated three times. **Measured, then recalibrated to a floor of six: six answers to MASTERED, from any `required_streak`** — the per-concept threshold no longer affects cost, because the score is already normalized against the concept's own difficulty and time threshold. A floor of **6** was then set deliberately: `MasteryEvidenceFloor` is a minimum on *how much* evidence, not a demand for six consecutive correct answers, and `internal/mastery/measure_test.go` measures the cost rather than assuming it — six profiles (perfect, slow, one-miss-in-eight, 80% correct, slow-but-right, starts-rough) against all eight real corpus `avg_time_seconds`, and every one masters in exactly 6, so median/p25/p75/min/max are all 6. **Amended 2026-10-05 — `cmd/latexfix` tripped `go-ai-lint` AIL070 ("byte iteration on string may break UTF-8 characters") at both scanners, and the fix is a robustness change rather than a bug fix, which is the interesting part.** `unescapeDelimiters` wrote one byte per iteration through `strings.Builder.WriteByte` and `textGroup` stepped `i` one byte at a time. I assumed both were corrupting non-ASCII and wrote a test asserting truncation — **the test passed against the unfixed code, so the assumption was wrong, and the failure is recorded here rather than deleted.** The byte-at-a-time form is lossless: `WriteByte` appends the raw byte and the loop visits every byte exactly once, so it reconstructs the input byte-for-byte (verified for em dashes, curly quotes, Greek letters and ellipses). And `textGroup` cannot match an opener mid-rune for a reason that is not in the code at all — a multi-byte UTF-8 sequence contains only bytes ≥ 0x80, every opener starts with a backslash at 0x5C, so a fragment starting mid-rune cannot begin with `\text{`. Both are now rune-at-a-time, which buys a guarantee that is local rather than resting on the encoding, but the honest description is *lint compliance*, not a data-loss fix. Two mechanical traps: the loops move `i` by hand (`i = j - 1`, `i += 2`), so converting them to `for i := range s` would **silently discard those jumps** — Go's range loop reassigns its index at the top of every iteration — and the row-break branch emits two bytes at `i` and `i+1` without a forward jump, so it has to write both together once the step becomes explicit. `TestBackslashCannotBeInsideAMultibyteRune` asserts the UTF-8 property the old reasoning depended on, so if it ever stops holding, the reasoning is revisited rather than assumed.

**Amended 2026-10-05 — two claims in the above were wrong, and both are recorded rather than quietly deleted.** (a) This ADR reported that "a sweep from 40% to 100% accuracy reports *every* level masterable and `MasteryThreshold` 0.6 is decorative". **It does not.** The sweep's test profile was `i%20 >= 12` — twelve wrong, then eight right — so a six-attempt window eventually landed entirely inside the clean run and reported mastery for a learner who was wrong 60% of the time. With evenly-distributed accuracy (`correctAt(i, n, k)` in `measure_test.go`) the threshold binds cleanly: **60%, 80% and 100% master in exactly 6 answers; 0%, 20%, 40% and 50% never master at any length.** `MasteryThreshold` 0.6 is kept unchanged — it was right, and the measurement was wrong. The lesson generalises past this file: *a probe's period is not the model*, and a synthetic pattern can manufacture a finding about the thing it is probing. (b) This ADR said variety "cannot veto". Precisely: the 0.15 bonus is large enough to flip the outcome **at the margin** — a 60%-accurate learner masters on varied instances and does not on identical ones — while leaving an 80%-accurate learner free to master on a generator that never varies. That is a *weight*, not a veto, and the property that matters is that no amount of demonstrated competence becomes **unreachable**; the `Instances >= 2` gate this replaced made a concept permanently unmasterable, which is a different and much worse failure. Both boundaries are now asserted rather than asserted-about: `TestVarietyRaisesTheBarWithoutBlockingMastery` and `TestMeasureAccuracyBandThatStillMasters`. The three knobs (`MasteryThreshold` 0.6, `MasteryEvidenceFloor` 6, the `varietyBonus` cap 0.15) sit together in one file. Floor of **6** is confirmed as the right gate for *leaving active teaching*: it is not a claim about durable retention, and the brief's own framing is that mastery means "demonstrated enough to exit the teaching loop". Making it larger on retention grounds would smuggle in the deferred spaced-retention system through the acquisition gate. The transition logic that ADR-038 left duplicated now has one authority: `Engine.applyGradedAttempt`, called by both `SubmitAnswer` and `submitAnswerWithTask`, so `Machine.Next` appears once in the codebase. Status still only moves forward, so mastery remains an exit. **Three things this change had to fix that were not in the brief.** (1) Difficulty was never persisted, so the server had strictly less evidence than the client: it computed one per question, passed it to the generator and dropped it. Now `attempts.difficulty` and `active_sessions.difficulty`, nullable and never backfilled, sourced from `activeSession.questionDifficulty` rather than the client; the NULL/non-NULL pair is covered by the ADR-012 parity test, which run against a real `postgres:16` caught two things a SQLite-only check cannot — a missing twelfth argument that made **every Postgres attempt write silently fail** (the test discarded the error with `_ =`), and a SQLite upsert left with `VALUES ()` by a regex assuming `$n` placeholders. (2) A gate on a derived signal made mastery **unreachable**: `EnoughForMastery` first also required two distinct problem instances, and a generator whose question text does not vary holds that at 1 forever. Variety now *scores* and cannot veto — a gate on a signal you do not fully control can lock a state. (3) The decay clock was gated on the rule being replaced: `LastReviewed` was set only on `streak >= required && avg time <= threshold`, a proxy for "a tier was just advanced" back when that was what advancement meant, so a concept could reach MASTERED with a streak of 1, leave `LastReviewed` nil, and read as fully decayed — the tests caught the ladder climbing **through DECAYING**. The clock now starts on tier attainment. The transition logic had also existed **twice**, `SubmitAnswer` and `submitAnswerWithTask`, one copy per answer host, and a quiz answer would have graded differently from a study answer; both now call `applyGradedAttempt`, leaving attempt-row recording and source classification to the host. `GetRecentAttemptsForConcept` is new because the window is read per graded answer and `GetAttemptsForStudent` returns everything. **Deferred:** spaced attainment *within* acquisition still needs a persisted `tier_attained_at`, as ADR-037 recorded; nothing here depends on it, and the ladder now makes that gap cheap to close later.
